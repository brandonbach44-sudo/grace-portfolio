Evaluating Expressions with Substitution

B3_9-18_9-22LessonPlans.docx

09/18, 09/22Equations & AlgebraLearning Support Math

PA Standards

CC.2.2.HS.D.9 / CC.2.2.7.EE.A.1 (foundational skill per IEP goals): Evaluate algebraic expressions by substituting given values for variables and applying order of operations.

Essential Questions

  • How do we evaluate an algebraic expression when variables are replaced with given values?
  • How does order of operations guide us in evaluating expressions accurately?

Objectives

  • Students will be able to order a set of numbers, including decimals and square roots, from largest to smallest.
  • Students will be able to substitute given values for variables in an algebraic expression, using parentheses around each substituted value.
  • Students will be able to apply order of operations to correctly evaluate an expression after substitution.
  • Students will be able to evaluate expressions involving exponents, absolute value, and multiple variables.

Assessment

  • Table-group ordering warm-up (checked together on the board)
  • Guided notes examples completed together
  • Independent notes practice problems (#3-12 and summary)
  • Completed practice worksheet on evaluating expressions
  • Completion of self-selected choice activity (color-by-number or mixed review worksheet)

Differentiation

  • Visual schedule posted and referenced to support transitions and predictability
  • Notes page chunked; work through examples together before releasing to independent practice
  • Reminder/anchor chart for order of operations (PEMDAS) available for reference
  • Substitution property reinforced concretely: 'put parentheses around the number every time you substitute'
  • Calculator use permitted as needed per IEP accommodations
  • Extended time provided for independent and practice worksheet portions
  • Student choice of closing activity (color-by-number vs. mixed practice) to support engagement and autonomy
  • Small-group/table support during warm-up and practice worksheet

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Visual schedule displayed at the start of class showing today's materials and sequence of activities, including the lunch break
  • Warm-up completed in table groups with an 8-minute visible timer to build routine and time-awareness
  • Clear verbal and visual cues signal transitions, especially around the lunch break
  • Consistent routine: warm-up → review → notes/guided practice → independent practice → lunch → review → practice worksheet → choice activity → closure
  • Positive reinforcement for on-task effort during independent work and choice activity

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • (10:47-10:50) Students enter, review the visual schedule on the board showing materials needed and today's activities.
  • (10:50-10:58) Warm-up: In table groups, students receive small cards with numbers (decimals, square roots, etc.) and order them from largest to smallest. An 8-minute timer is displayed.
  • (10:58-11:05) Re
  • view the warm-up together as a class on the board, discussing strategies for comparing decimals and square roots.

Materials Needed

  • Visual schedule (board/projector)
  • Warm-up number-ordering cards (decimals, square roots, etc.) — one set per table group
  • Visible timer
  • Evaluating Expressions guided notes (All Things Algebra)
  • Whiteboard/projector for modeling examples
  • Independent practice worksheet (additional evaluating expressions problems)
  • Choice activity: color-by-number order of operations worksheet OR mixed practice worksheet (evaluating expressions, absolute value, ordering numbers, order of operations)
  • Pencils, calculators (per accommodations)

Vocabulary

  • Variable — a letter that represents an unknown or changing number
  • Substitution — replacing a variable with a given number
  • Evaluate — to find the value of an expression
  • Order of operations (PEMDAS) — the order in which operations must be performed
  • Exponent — review
  • Absolute value — review

Lesson Sequence

  • (10:47-10:50) Students enter, review the visual schedule on the board showing materials needed and today's activities.
  • (10:50-10:58) Warm-up: In table groups, students receive small cards with numbers (decimals, square roots, etc.) and order them from largest to smallest. An 8-minute timer is displayed.
  • (10:58-11:05) Re
  • view the warm-up together as a class on the board, discussing strategies for comparing decimals and square roots.
  • (11:05-11:11) Pass out Evaluating Expressions notes. Introduce the substitution property: "If a variable is replaced with a number, we substitute that value in for the variable." Emphasize: "Each time you substitute a variable with a number, put parentheses around the number!" Review that students must follow order of operations to evaluate. Model example 1 (ab^2 + c).
  • (11:11-11:17) Model example 2 (3x^2 - 4x), thinking aloud through substitution, parentheses, and order of operations at each step.
  • (11:17-11:23) Guided practice: work through 'You Try' problems 3-4 together as a class, checking for understanding.
  • (11:23-11:29) Students begin independent practice on problems 5 and beyond; work will continue after lunch.
  • LUNCH BREAK ---
  • (upon return, ~5 min) Students return, review visual schedule to re-orient to the remainder of class.
  • (12:05-12:20) Students finish independent practice on the notes page (problems 5-12 and summary); review answers together as a class, addressing common errors.
  • (12:20-12:25) Pass out and explain the new practice worksheet with additional evaluating expressions problems.
  • (12:25-12:46) Students work independently (or with table support) on the practice worksheet.
  • (remaining time) Choice activity: students select either a color-by-number order of operations activity or a mixed practice worksheet covering evaluating expressions, absolute value, ordering numbers, and order of operations, based on preference and need.

Closure

  • Bring the class back together and review 1-2 problems as a quick exit check.
  • Recap the big idea: substitute carefully with parentheses, then follow order of operations to evaluate.
  • Preview that the next lesson will continue building on evaluating expressions and/or order of operations.
  • Collect notes, practice worksheet, and choice activity.
