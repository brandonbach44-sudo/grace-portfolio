03/11Learning Support Math

PA Standards

CC.2.2.8.B.2: Understand the connections between proportional relationships, lines, and linear equations, including slope and y-intercept.

Essential Questions

  • How can I use the slope formula to find the slope between two points?
  • What is slope-intercept form, and what do the slope and y-intercept tell me?
  • How can I check that my slope answer makes sense?

Objectives

  • Students will be able to:
  • Use the slope formula with two coordinates to find the slope.
  • Complete a slope practice worksheet on their own.
  • Copy slope-intercept form notes.
  • Review slope-intercept form by playing Gimkit, if we have time.

Assessment

  • Formative: Whiteboards during the slope game: I see which students find the slope correctly and how fast.
  • Formative: Slope practice worksheet, with my observation and help as I circulate.
  • Formative: Student responses as we go through the slope-intercept form notes.
  • Formative: Gimkit results on slope-intercept form, if we have time.

Differentiation

  • Supports: The visual schedule is on the board, and students have the materials they need (whiteboard, marker, eraser, calculator).
  • Supports: Students use calculators for the slope formula.
  • Process: Students try the slope worksheet on their own, and I circulate to help those who need it.
  • Content: Notes are on the board for students to copy, so everyone has the same notes.
  • IEP/504: Accommodations in each student’s IEP/504 plan will be followed.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • The visual schedule is on the board when students enter.
  • Students grab a whiteboard, marker, eraser, and calculator before we start.
  • Students take turns in the slope game, two at a time.
  • Students work independently on the slope worksheet while I circulate.
  • After lunch we take notes, and the class plays a slope-intercept Gimkit if there is time.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Students have learned the slope formula and slope-intercept form. The slope game is a fun way to practice the formula, and the worksheet gives independent practice. After lunch, the notes introduce slope-intercept form, and Gimkit gives review if there is time.

Materials Needed

Visual schedule (board), whiteboards, markers, erasers, calculators, slide with slope answers, slope practice worksheet, slope-intercept form notes, pencils, Gimkit (slope-intercept), student devices

Vocabulary

Slope; slope formula; coordinates; y-intercept; slope-intercept form; graph; equation

Lesson Sequence

  • Arrival
  • The visual schedule is on the board.
  • Students grab a whiteboard, marker, eraser, and calculator. Once they have their materials, we start.
  • Warm-Up: Slope Game — 10–15 minutes
  • A slide shows multiple different slope answers.
  • Two students play at a time. They get coordinates and use the slope formula to find the slope.
  • The first student to get the answer walks up to the board and touches or circles their answer, and wins the round.
  • We continue until we are out of answers.
  • Slope Practice Worksheet
  • Students try the slope practice worksheet on their own.
  • I circulate to help.
  • Lunch
  • After Lunch: Slope-Intercept Form Notes
  • Students take notes on slope-intercept form.
  • Slope-Intercept Gimkit
  • If we have time, we play a slope-intercept Gimkit.

Closure

  • At the end of class, I look at the worksheets and any Gimkit results to see who knows slope and slope-intercept form and who needs more practice.
  • Connection to Next Lesson: Students keep practicing slope and slope-intercept form.
