One- & Two-Step Equations

10/01, 10/02Equations & AlgebraLearning Support Math

PA Standards

CC.2.2.7.EE.B.4 (foundational skill per IEP goals): Solve real-life and mathematical problems using numerical and algebraic equations, including one-step and two-step linear equations.

Essential Questions

  • How do we solve one-step and two-step equations?
  • What steps help us eliminate fractions when solving equations?

Objectives

  • Students will review solving one-step equations.
  • Students will learn to solve two-step equations.
  • Students will apply strategies to eliminate fractions when solving equations.
  • Students will work through 'tricky' equation examples that require extra attention.
  • Students will begin independent practice solving two-step equations.

Assessment

  • IXL progress-monitoring data
  • One-step equation review responses (4 questions)
  • Guided notes completion (one-step review section, two-step equations, fraction elimination, tricky examples)
  • Participation during guided examples and tricky examples discussion
  • Progress on the two-step equations practice worksheet

Differentiation

  • Visual schedule and timer displayed during IXL to support pacing and predictability
  • One-step review provided both on the board and on paper for multiple modalities
  • Notes include a built-in one-step review section before introducing two-step equations, to scaffold from familiar to new content
  • 'Tricky examples' placed last intentionally, once students are comfortable with standard two-step problems, so they don't get thrown off early
  • All new examples worked through together as a class before independent practice begins
  • Reduced problem set or extended time on the practice worksheet as needed (worksheet is not expected to be finished in class)

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Visual schedule and timer displayed during IXL
  • Clear verbal and visual cues signal transitions, especially around the lunch break
  • Consistent routine: IXL → one-step review → notes/new content → lunch → guided practice → independent practice
  • Positive reinforcement for engaging with the harder 'tricky' examples

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • (0-15 min) IXL for progress monitoring, with the visual schedule and a timer displayed on the board.
  • (15-23 min) One-step equation review: 4 questions displayed on the board, with students completing them on paper.

Materials Needed

  • Visual schedule (board/projector)
  • Timer
  • Devices for IXL
  • One-step equation review sheet (4 questions)
  • Two-step equation guided notes (including a one-step review section and a section on eliminating fractions)
  • Two-step equations practice worksheet
  • Pencils, calculators

Vocabulary

  • One-step equation — review
  • Two-step equation — an equation that requires two operations to solve for the variable
  • Inverse operations — operations that undo each other (e.g., addition/subtraction, multiplication/division)
  • Eliminate/clear fractions — a strategy to remove fractions from an equation before solving

Lesson Sequence

  • (23-35 min) Distribute two-step equation notes. Review the one-step equations section included in the notes.
  • (35-45 min) Introduce how to 'get rid of' fractions when solving equations.
  • LUNCH ---
  • (45-65 min) Two-step equations: work through all examples together as a class.
  • (65-75 min) Work through the 'tricky examples' section of the notes last, once students are comfortable with standard two-step problems.
  • (75-90 min) Begin the two-step equations practice worksheet; students will not finish by the end of class.

Closure

  • Collect notes and the partially completed practice worksheet (to be finished during a future class or as homework, per teacher preference).
  • Briefly recap the steps for solving a two-step equation.
