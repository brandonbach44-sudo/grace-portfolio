Staying Organized: Keeping an Agenda

Organization & Self-AdvocacyEssential Skills and Strategies

PA Standards

  • PA Career Education and Work Standard 13.3.11.B:
  • Demonstrate professional skills in the workplace, including appropriate work habits, organization, and time management.

Essential Questions

  • Why is staying organized important?
  • How can staying organized help me succeed in school?
  • How can using an agenda help me remember important tasks and events?

Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to identify at least two reasons why staying organized is important and use an agenda or to-do list to record at least three tasks, assignments, or important events.

Assessment

  • Formative Assessment
  • Students will:
  • Verbally identify one reason organization is important.
  • Identify one way an agenda can help them.
  • Identify at least two things they could record in an agenda.
  • Use their mini agenda to record at least three personal or school-related tasks/events.
  • Check PowerSchool/Canvas and add at least one upcoming or missing assignment, if applicable.

Differentiation

  •  Provide information through multiple formats, including verbal explanations, visual examples, and hands-on practice.
  •  Provide students with a physical agenda while also demonstrating digital options.
  •  Provide additional processing time and repeat or rephrase directions as needed.
  •  Model how to enter an assignment into an agenda before asking students to complete the task independently.
  •  Break the task into smaller steps: identify the task → identify the due date → write the task → check that it was recorded correctly.
  •  Provide one-on-one prompting or teacher assistance when needed.
  •  Allow students to use their existing classroom supports and IEP accommodations.
  •  Provide examples that are relevant to students' lives, such as homework, work schedules, sports, appointments, birthdays, and school activities.
  •  For students who do not regularly receive homework, allow them to record personal responsibilities, activities, or upcoming events.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • The lesson will follow the established classroom routines and behavioral expectations already used by the cooperating teacher.
  • At the beginning of the lesson, students will be informed of the learning objective and expectations for participation.
  • The teacher will:
  • Provide clear, concise directions.
  • Use a visual schedule/lesson sequence.
  • Provide positive reinforcement for participation and effort.
  • Use proximity and quiet redirection when needed.
  • Allow students appropriate processing time.
  • Provide choices when appropriate.
  • Maintain predictable transitions between activities.
  • Use students' established behavior supports and IEP accommodations.
  • Maintain a respectful and supportive classroom environment.

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • Ask students what they have used to stay organized so far throughout school
  • They have been taught to use binders and folders to keep papers in one spot
  • They get schedules at the beginning of the year so that they know where they are going and what time they need to be there
  • Ask them where they typically find assignments (canvas, powerschool)

Materials Needed

  • Laptop
  • Projector/interactive board
  • "Staying Organized: Keeping an Agenda" slideshow
  • Pencils/pens
  • Sample assignments/tasks
  • Students' individual PowerSchool/Canvas accounts
  • Optional: printed sample calendar/to-do list

Vocabulary

  • Agenda: A personal list of tasks or events that need to be completed/remembered
  • Organization: arranging or planning things in a logical order (by putting them into a schedule, agenda, etc.)
  • To-do list: A list of tasks that need to be completed.
  • Calendar: A tool used to keep track of dates, events, and deadlines.
  • Due date: The date something needs to be completed or turned in.

Lesson Sequence

  • Allow students to get settled and review the visual schedule for the lesson.
  • Introduce the lesson objective: "Today we are going to learn how staying organized can help us in school and after high school."
  • Display the question: "Why is staying organized important?"
  • Allow students to share their ideas verbally.
  • Discuss how organization can help students remember responsibilities, reduce stress, manage their time, and complete tasks on time.
  • Display the question: "How can an agenda help me stay organized?"
  • Allow students to share their ideas.
  • Show video on Keeping an Agenda
  • Explain that an agenda can be used to record assignments, due dates, tests, events, work schedules, appointments, and other important information.
  • Model how to take a sample assignment and record it in an agenda.
  • Ask students what information should be included when writing an assignment in an agenda.
  • Introduce four organizational tools:
  • To-do lists
  • Agenda books
  • Calendars
  • Online scheduling/planning resources
  • Discuss when and how each tool can be useful.
  • Ask students which organizational tool they think would work best for them and why.
  • Provide each student with a mini agenda.
  • Give students several sample tasks/events and have them practice recording them in their agenda.
  • Have students check PowerSchool/Canvas for upcoming or missing assignments.
  • Have students record appropriate assignments or responsibilities in their agenda.
  • Circulate and provide prompts, feedback, and assistance as needed.
  • Ask students to review their agenda and check that they have included the necessary information.
  • Have students share one thing they plan to use their agenda for.

Closure

  • Review why keeping an agenda can help the students stay organized and get their work done on time, or help them remember any special dates or events
  • Allow them to use the rest of the time to check PowerSchool to see if they have any missing or upcoming assignments that they can work on for the rest of class
  • Encourage them to write down anything they did not complete in a to do list or agenda
