Identifying Triggers

ESS9-15LessonPlan.docx

Also in: ESS/weeks 1+2+3/Lesson Plans

09/15Social & Emotional SkillsEssential Skills

PA Standards

PA Career Education and Work Standards (effective July 1, 2026): 13.3.11.A — Evaluate personal attitudes and work habits that support career retention and advancement. PA Career Ready Skills: Self-Awareness and Self-Management — recognize feelings and respond effectively to challenges.

Essential Questions

How can knowing my triggers help me choose a strategy before a situation becomes harder to manage?

Objectives

  • Define a trigger as something that can cause a sudden emotional response.
  • Identify personal situations or events that may lead to irritation, upset, anger, sadness, or another strong emotional response.
  • Rank three common triggers by frequency or intensity and identify a strategy that may help in a school setting.

Assessment

Formative: responses during the whole-group discussion, teacher questioning, and observation of student participation. Product evidence: completed trigger list with a top-three ranking and at least one appropriate school-based strategy for the highest-priority trigger.

Differentiation

Normalize a range of emotions while maintaining the expectation that students are responsible for their actions. Offer examples and non-personal hypothetical situations so students can participate without disclosing sensitive information. Allow students to write, type, select from choices, or respond verbally. Provide a partially completed organizer, sentence starters, visual strategy choices, and private teacher support. Students ready for enrichment may identify multiple strategies and explain when each might be useful.

Classroom Environment & Routines

Use a calm, predictable structure and remind students that sharing is optional. Establish a respectful discussion norm: no laughing at, judging, or repeating another student's personal information. Students may pass or use a hypothetical example. Teacher models neutral language and focuses on problem-solving rather than blame.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Ask students to recall yesterday's idea of identifying strengths and areas for growth. Introduce the idea that self-awareness also includes noticing situations that make it harder to use our skills. Give a neutral example, such as being asked to stop a preferred activity unexpectedly, and ask what feelings or actions might occur.

Materials Needed

Daily check-in form; trigger/strategy graphic organizer or paper; projector/display; pencils/computers; optional emotion scale or visual feelings chart; strategy choice cards.

Vocabulary

Trigger, emotion, behavior, irritation, upset, strategy, coping strategy, self-management, intensity, frequency

Lesson Sequence

  • 0–5 min — Daily check-in: Students complete the routine. Teacher briefly identifies any classroom needs that may affect participation.
  • 5–20 min — Group engagement activity: Complete a short, low-risk activity about emotions or self-management, such as matching common situations with possible feelings. Emphasize that different people can have different reactions to the same event.
  • 20-30 min — Direct instruction: Explain that triggers are events or situations that can lead to a sudden emotional response. Reinforce: emotions are not 'bad' or 'wrong,' but students remain responsible for how they respond or act.
  • 30-35 min — Model: Teacher models a hypothetical trigger, identifies the feeling/response, and demonstrates choosing a strategy.
  • 35-45 min — Independent reflection: Students list situations that can make them irritated, upset, angry, sad, or otherwise emotionally activated. Students may use personal or hypothetical examples.
  • 45-55 min — Prioritize: Students identify their top three triggers based on how often they occur or how strongly they affect them. The highest-priority trigger is labeled #1.
  • 55-65 min — Strategy planning: Next to the #1 trigger, students identify what they typically experience and one strategy they can use at school. Teacher conferences and helps students choose realistic strategies.
  • 65–75 min — Share/transition: Volunteers share a general strategy, not a personal trigger. Students clean up materials.

Closure

Students complete: 'When I notice ___, I can try ___.' Reinforce that recognizing a trigger is a first step toward making a more intentional choice about a response. Preview that the next lesson will focus on meeting new people and starting conversations.
