Slides: https://canva.link/cxs74917xw2djqq Your Actions Affect Others Do your daily check-in Introduce the lesson with a group question: Can you think of something someone has done to make your day better? What did they do? How did it make you feel? Allow students to answer verbally Explain that our actions affect others Everything we say and do can affect the people around us. Our words can make someone feel happy, appreciated, included, or supported. Our words can also make someone feel upset, embarrassed, frustrated, or left out. Even small actions can make a big difference. Introduce empathy Explain what it is: Empathy is the ability to understand or consider how another person might be feeling. Before you act, it is important to consider how someone else may feel as a result of your actions Examples listed on the slide Video on empathy Remind students that their actions affect others and ask them questions based on the video: How can you tell how someone else might be feeling? What are some ways you can show kindness to others? Why is it important to think about other people's feelings? Remind students the importance of thinking before you act/speak Is what I am saying respectful? How might the other person feel? Could my actions hurt or upset someone? Is there a kinder or more helpful way to respond? Remind students that sometimes you hurt others feelings without realizing it, we all make mistakes sometimes, even when we don't know it will upset someone else Sometimes we say or do things without thinking. We can take responsibility for our actions. We can apologize when appropriate. We can try to make things better. Teach a simple apology: "I'm sorry I ____. I understand that it may have made you feel ____. Next time, I will try to ____." It is okay to make mistakes, but always remember to think about how other people may feel and decide whether it is an appropriate situation for an apology Remember to ask yourself if what you are about to say or do is kind and respectful Work on missing assignments
