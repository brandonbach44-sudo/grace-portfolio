Executive Functioning Self-Assessment

09/14Organization & Self-AdvocacyEssential Skills

PA Standards

PA Career Education and Work Standards (effective July 1, 2026): 13.3.11.A — Evaluate personal attitudes and work habits that support career retention and advancement; 13.2.9-12.E — Evaluate time and project management strategies and their application to both personal and work situations.

Essential Questions

What executive functioning skills do I use well, and which skills are areas I can improve?

Objectives

  • Complete the Executive Functioning (EF) self-assessment on pages 6–8 of the provided ESS Executive Functioning Packet.
  • Rate and reflect on current executive functioning skills using the rating system provided in the packet.
  • Identify at least one executive functioning strength and one skill that could be improved.

Assessment

Formative: teacher observation during the check-in, whole-group engagement, and quiz completion; questioning during discussion. Product evidence: completed EF quiz/self-rating on pages 6–8 and a brief verbal or written reflection identifying one strength and one area for growth.

Differentiation

Provide the packet digitally or on paper; read directions aloud and clarify unfamiliar vocabulary without changing the intended self-rating. Chunk pages into smaller sections and provide one item at a time as needed. Allow students to respond verbally, point to choices, type, or use assistive technology. Provide extended time and frequent check-ins. Use a visual agenda and explicit modeling. Students ready for enrichment can identify a specific school/work situation in which improving one skill would help.

Classroom Environment & Routines

Begin with the established daily check-in routine. Display a visual agenda and learning target. Review expectations: participate respectfully, answer honestly, and remember that the self-assessment is for identifying strengths and growth areas, not for getting a 'right' or 'wrong' score. Circulate during independent work, offer quiet redirection, and provide breaks when needed.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Connect to students’ existing routines by asking: 'What is one thing you do every day at school that helps you stay on track?' Briefly explain that executive functioning skills include planning, organization, self-monitoring, and managing tasks. Preview that today's quiz is a chance to notice current strengths and areas for growth.

Materials Needed

Daily check-in form; ESS Executive Functioning Packet, pages 6–8; pencils; computers/tablets if using a digital copy; projector/display; optional highlighters or sticky notes.

Vocabulary

Executive functioning; self-assessment; self-reflection; organization; planning; self-monitoring; time management; task completion; strength; growth area.

Lesson Sequence

  • 0–5 min — Daily check-in: Students complete the established daily check-in. Teacher briefly reviews any immediate needs and reminds students of the objective.
  • 5–20 min — Whole-group engagement: Prompt students to name or identify examples of executive functioning skills they use at school. Teacher records examples and connects them to the lesson.
  • 20-25 min — Introduce the lesson: Explain that students will complete an EF self-assessment and that honest answers are more useful than answers they think the teacher wants.
  • 25-55 min — EF quiz/self-rating: Students complete pages 6–8 of the ESS Executive Functioning Packet. Teacher models how to interpret the rating scale and circulates to clarify directions and provide individualized support.
  • 55-60 min — Review and reflection: Students review their responses and identify one area they feel confident about and one area they would like to improve. Students may share verbally, write, or type.
  • 60-65 min — Individual teacher check-ins: Teacher briefly conferences with students to connect a growth area to a real school situation.
  • 65-75 min — Transition/cleanup: Students turn in or save their completed work and prepare for dismissal.

Closure

Students complete: 'One executive functioning skill I am proud of is ___, and one skill I want to work on is ___.' Preview that the class will use self-awareness to examine situations that can make it harder to stay regulated and successful.
