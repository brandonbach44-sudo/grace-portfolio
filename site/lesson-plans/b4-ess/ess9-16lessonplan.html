09/16Social & Emotional SkillsEssential Skills

PA Standards

PA Career Education and Work Standards (effective July 1, 2026): 13.2.9-12.B — Use professional etiquette and observe social protocols when communicating verbally, nonverbally, and in writing; 13.2.9-12.C — Demonstrate shared responsibility for collaborative work and the value of contributions made by team members with various perspectives.

Essential Questions

How can I start a conversation, show interest, and end a conversation appropriately?

Objectives

  • :
  • Identify at least two appropriate ways to start a conversation with someone new.
  • Practice asking a question, listening to the response, and using a follow-up statement or question.
  • Identify at least two behaviors that help conversations remain respectful and appropriate.

Assessment

Formative: teacher observation during whole-group discussion and partner practice; questioning and feedback during role-play. Performance evidence: completed Student Profile and participation in a brief conversation practice using an appropriate greeting, question, listening response, and closing.

Differentiation

Provide the Student Profile in paper and digital formats. Read choices aloud and allow students to select, write, type, or dictate responses. Provide visual conversation cards with sentence starters and examples. Pair students thoughtfully and allow practice with the teacher or a trusted adult if peer interaction is uncomfortable. Model both appropriate and inappropriate examples without singling out students. For enrichment, students can practice follow-up questions and adapting communication to a workplace setting.

Classroom Environment & Routines

Use predictable partner routines and explicitly teach respectful boundaries. Students may choose a partner when appropriate or be assigned one. Establish that students do not need to disclose private information. Teacher monitors interactions, provides feedback, and redirects off-topic or inappropriate comments respectfully.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Ask: 'What makes it easier or harder to talk to someone you do not know?' Acknowledge that ice-breakers can feel uncomfortable but explain that they provide a structured way to find common interests. Connect the skill to school, community, employment, and other real-life settings.

Materials Needed

Daily check-in form, Student Profile handout, conversation starter/follow-up cards, projector/display, pencils/computers, optional whiteboard table or paper.

Vocabulary

Conversation, introduction, commonality, active listening, follow-up question, interest, respectful, personal information, social protocol, workplace etiquette.

Lesson Sequence

  • 0–5 min — Daily check-in: Students complete the routine and prepare materials.
  • 5–20 min — Daily engagement activity: Brief partner or whole-group prompt about a favorite interest, activity, food, or entertainment choice.
  • 20-30 min — Whole-group discussion: Explain why meeting new people can be challenging and why structured ice-breakers can help people find common interests.
  • 30-40 min — Student Profile: Students complete the profile with information they are comfortable sharing: name, interests, favorite food/music, strengths, something they want to learn, favorite place, and something that makes them happy.
  • 40-45 min — Teacher modeling: Model a short conversation: greeting → question → listening response → follow-up → closing. Highlight phrases such as 'That's cool,' 'I like that too,' and 'Tell me more about that.'
  • 45-55 min — Partner practice: Students turn to a classmate and practice introducing themselves and asking questions from the profile. Teacher circulates, prompts students as needed, and gives specific feedback.
  • 55-65 min — Whole-group reflection: Discuss what helps a conversation go well and what should generally be avoided, such as interrupting, asking overly personal questions, making rude comments, or sharing someone else's private information.
  • 65-75 min — Closing practice: Each student identifies one conversation skill they can use again.

Closure

Students state or select one skill they practiced: starting a conversation, asking a question, listening, showing interest, or ending a conversation. Preview that the next lesson will involve communicating with families and school staff through the weekly Student-Led Communication email.
