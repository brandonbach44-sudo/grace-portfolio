11/17
Tuesday: What your emotions mean Slides: https://canva.link/rsou158kbhv7dam Daily Check-In Group Activity: Identifying emotion GimKit: https://www.gimkit.com/view/6942dc8c23fd52aa62c98a9c Inside out: https://youtu.be/dOkyKyVFnSs?si=pZi750JhUh4g3F5k What is she feeling? Give students a moment to answer each and discuss why she is feeling that way Introduce the lesson: What your emotions mean Emotions are signals Emotions tell us what is going on inside our body in response to the outside environment Happiness can tell us that something feels enjoyable, exciting, or meaningful. Example: You get a good grade on a test or spend time with friends. Sadness can tell us that something matters to us, that we miss something, or that we need support. Example: A friend moves away or you don't get invited somewhere. Anger can tell us that something feels unfair, frustrating, or that a boundary has been crossed. Example: Someone interrupts you or you feel like you aren't being listened to. These emotions can tell us that we feel uncertain, nervous, or concerned about something. Example: You have to give a presentation or try something new. Surprise tells us that something unexpected has happened. Example: Someone plans a surprise party or you receive unexpected news. Important point to teach: There are no bad emotions. All emotions are normal. However, we are responsible for how we respond to our emotions. Give students different real-life scenarios. Have them identify the emotion and discuss what that emotion might be telling the person. Scenario Slides: Scenario: You studied for a test and received a good grade. Question to discuss: what might you feel? Why? Your friend makes plans and doesn't invite you What might you feel? What is that emotion telling you? You have a big assignment due tomorrow, but you haven’t started What might you feel? What is that emotion telling you? Someone takes something from your desk without telling you What might you feel? For each scenario, ask: What emotion might this person be feeling? What happened to cause this emotion? What might this emotion be telling them? What could they do next? Remind students that different people feel different ways in all situations, it’s important to recognize how you feel and think about what it may be telling you about the situation.
