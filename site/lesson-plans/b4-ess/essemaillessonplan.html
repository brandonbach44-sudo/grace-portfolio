Staying Organized: Managing Email

Organization & Self-AdvocacyESS/Executive Functioning

PA Standards

  • Career Education and Work Standards – 13.3.11.A:
  • Evaluate personal interests and skills in relation to career options.
  • 13.3.11.E:
  • Demonstrate, in the workplace, the use of appropriate communication skills.
  • 13.3.11.F:
  • Demonstrate appropriate work habits and behaviors.

Essential Questions

  • Why is it important to keep my emails organized?
  • How can I keep my email organized?
  • How can I appropriately email my teachers when I turn in late work?
  • How can an organized email inbox help me at school and work?

Objectives

  • Students will identify at least two reasons why keeping an email inbox organized is important.
  • Students will identify whether an email should be deleted, replied to, or acted upon when given common school/work-related examples.
  • Students will identify at least two strategies for maintaining an organized email inbox.
  • Given a school-based email scenario and a template/checklist, students will compose an organized email containing the necessary information, with support as needed.
  • Students will use a checklist to review an email before sending it.

Assessment

  • Formative Assessment
  • Students will:
  • Verbally identify one reason organization is important.
  • Identify one reason keeping an email organized is important
  • Identify at least two way they can keep their emails organized
  • Students will share when they can delete emails or reply to emails
  • Students will be able to use a template to write their Student-Led Communication email to their case managers and parents which will be done weekly
  • Students will use a checklist to make sure they are adding a subject line, proof reading their emails, inserting a picture of their grades into their emails
  • Check PowerSchool/Canvas and finish any missing work, use the template for late work if they are turning in anything passed the due date

Differentiation

  • Email templates with word banks for greeting and closing options
  • Checklists to ensure students are not missing any steps (subject line, proof reading, etc)
  • Allow students to use voice to text to compose emails
  • Provide one-on-one prompting or teacher assistance when needed.
  • Allow students to use their existing classroom supports and IEP accommodations.
  • Provide examples that are relevant to students' lives, such
  • Present one concept at a time using concise, concrete language.
  • Read example emails aloud when needed.
  • Provide additional processing/wait time.
  • Repeat, rephrase, or chunk directions as needed.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • The teacher will:
  • Provide clear, concise directions.
  • Use a visual schedule/lesson sequence.
  • Provide positive reinforcement for participation and effort.
  • Use proximity and quiet redirection when needed.
  • Allow students appropriate processing time.
  • Provide choices when appropriate.
  • Maintain predictable transitions between activities.
  • Use students' established behavior supports and IEP accommodations.
  • Maintain a respectful and supportive classroom environment

Activating Prior Knowledge

Begin with Slide 1, 'Staying Organized: Managing Your Email.' Ask students what they know about email, what they use it for, and how they currently organize their inbox. Connect the lesson to school, work, college/training programs, and everyday life. Explain that students will learn a repeatable routine for managing emails they receive and sending organized emails themselves.

Materials Needed

  • Laptop
  • Projector/interactive board
  • "Staying Organized: Email" slideshow
  • Sample assignments/tasks
  • Students' individual PowerSchool/Canvas accounts
  • Printed email templates with word banks
  • Printed checklists
  • Example email scenarios

Vocabulary

  • Email – An electronic message.
  • Inbox – The place where received emails are stored.
  • Reply – To send a response to an email.
  • Delete – To remove an email that is no longer needed.
  • Spam – Unwanted or potentially harmful email.
  • Action – Something you need to do because of information in an email.

Lesson Sequence

  • 1. Introduction / Prior Knowledge – 3 minutes: Display the title slide. Ask what students know about email and how they currently organize emails. Introduce the lesson goal.
  • 2. Why Email Organization Matters – 5–7 minutes: Use Slide 2. Allow students to answer before explaining. Discuss classwork, schedule changes, meetings, RAMS time, job interviews, work schedules, and other important communication. Ask what could happen if an important email is missed.
  • 3. How to Keep Email Organized – 5 minutes: Use Slide 3. Discuss deleting unnecessary emails, starring important messages, creating folders when useful, and writing down actions that need to be completed.
  • 4. Introduce the Three Choices – 3 minutes: Use Slide 4. Introduce DELETE, REPLY, and TAKE ACTION. Explain that some emails may require more than one step.
  • 5. When to Delete – 3 minutes: Use Slide 5. Discuss old/unneeded emails, spam, advertisements, and emails related to completed tasks. Emphasize that important information should not be deleted just to make the inbox smaller.
  • 6. When to Reply – 3 minutes: Use Slide 6. Discuss responding when someone asks a question, needs confirmation, or when the student has a follow-up question. Explain that not every email needs a reply.
  • 7. When to Take Action – 3 minutes: Use Slide 7. Discuss completing/turning something in, adding a date to a calendar, getting something signed, bringing something in, or completing another task.
  • 8. Guided Practice – 6–8 minutes: Use Slides 8–11. Read each example aloud and allow students to decide whether they should reply, delete, or take action. Ask students to explain their reasoning.
  • 9. Transition to Sending Emails – 1–2 minutes: Explain that staying organized also means managing emails students send. Introduce the student-led communication checklist, word bank, and late-work email template.
  • 10. Student-Led Communication Emails – 3 minutes: Review the checklist. Discuss including a greeting, purpose, important information, questions, appropriate language, name, and checking the recipient before sending.
  • 11. Email Word Bank and Template – 3 minutes: Explain how the word bank provides phrases for greetings, explaining why they are emailing, asking questions, and closing. Model how the late-work template can be used to tell a teacher that an assignment has been submitted.
  • Writing the email: 10 minutes: allow students to use templates and write their student led communication emails to their case managers/parents
  • 13.

Closure

  • 3–5 minutes: Review DELETE, REPLY, and TAKE ACTION. Ask students to explain one reason email organization is important and one strategy they can use. Complete the exit ticket (verbally)
  • Closure:
  • Review the three main email decisions: DELETE when an email is no longer needed, REPLY when someone needs an answer or confirmation, and TAKE ACTION when the email requires the student to do something. Reinforce that students can also use checklists, word banks, templates, calendars, and folders as organizational supports.
  • Exit Ticket (verbal answers):
  • 1. Name one reason it is important to keep your email organized.
  • 2. What should you do if someone emails you asking a question?
  • 3. What should you do if an email tells you to complete a form?
  • 4. Name one strategy you can use to keep your email organized.
  • 5. What is one thing you should check before sending an email?
