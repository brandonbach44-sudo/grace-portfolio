Writing a Professional Email

PA Standards

PA Career Education and Work Standards (effective July 1, 2026): 13.2.9-12.B — Use professional etiquette and observe social protocols when communicating verbally, nonverbally, and in writing; 13.3.11.A — Evaluate personal attitudes and work habits that support career retention and advancement.

Essential Questions

How can I communicate about my progress in a clear, respectful, and responsible way?

Objectives

  • :
  • Access PowerSchool and obtain a current screenshot of grades.
  • Locate the Student-Led Communication email and use Reply All to communicate with the appropriate recipients.
  • Write one thing the student is proud of and one area the student plans to work on, and attach/paste the grade screenshot.
  • Star the email thread so it can be located easily for future weekly check-ins.

Assessment

Summative/product assessment: completed Student-Led Communication email. The weekly task is worth 5 points when all required components are completed on Thursday; 4 points when completed on the next school day after a reminder; 3 points when completed during the additional make-up day (typically Monday); after Monday, the assignment receives a zero according to the classroom procedure. Teacher checks for the required screenshot, Reply All, reflection statements, and starred thread.

Differentiation

Provide the previously supplied email template and Canvas example. Use a projected model and complete each step one at a time. Provide a checklist and allow students to work from a printed or digital checklist. Support students with reading, typing, navigation, or organization needs while maintaining student ownership of the message. Allow speech-to-text or other approved assistive technology. For enrichment, students can independently review the tone, clarity, and completeness of their message before sending.

Classroom Environment & Routines

Follow the established Thursday routine consistently. Display a visual checklist and model the process without displaying private student grades or email content. Remind students that the email is a school communication and should use respectful, school-appropriate language. Teacher circulates to provide navigation and writing support while encouraging independence.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Review Wednesday's focus on respectful communication and connect it to written communication. Ask: 'What makes an email to a teacher or family member different from a text to a friend?' Briefly identify professional tone, complete information, and appropriate recipients as key differences.

Materials Needed

Daily check-in form, Student computer, powerschool, gmail, student-led communication email template, grade screenshot, teacher checklist/rubric, projector display

Vocabulary

Professional communication, Reply All, recipient, screenshot, reflection, progress, goal, tone, email thread, star.

Lesson Sequence

  • 0–5 min — Daily check-in: Students complete the routine.
  • 5–15min — Review objective and expectations: Explain that Thursday's task is a graded weekly communication routine designed to build independence and self-advocacy.
  • 15-25 min — Teacher model: Demonstrate the process using a non-student example: open PowerSchool → view grades → take screenshot → open Gmail → find the Student-Led Communication email → select Reply All.
  • 25-50 min — Student work time: Students complete the process independently as able. Teacher provides step-by-step prompts only when needed.
  • 50-55 min — Write the reflection: Students state one thing they are proud of and one thing they plan to work on. Students may use the provided template and Canvas example.
  • 55-60 min — Insert screenshot and proofread: Students paste the grade screenshot into the email and check that the message includes all required parts and uses appropriate language.
  • 60-65 min — Send and star: Students send the completed email and click the star on the email thread so it is easy to find each week.
  • 65-75 min — Teacher verification/closure: Teacher checks completion and gives immediate feedback or identifies the next step for students who need assistance.

Closure

Students complete a quick checklist: 'I checked my grades; I wrote my reflection; I included my screenshot; I used Reply All; I starred the thread.' Reinforce that this routine builds independence in communicating about school progress.
