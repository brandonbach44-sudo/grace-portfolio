Friday Organization Reset

ESS9-18LessonPlan.docx

Also in: ESS/weeks 1+2+3/Lesson Plans

09/18Organization & Self-AdvocacyEssential Skills

PA Standards

PA Career Education and Work Standards (effective July 1, 2026): 13.2.9-12.E — Evaluate time and project management strategies and their application to both personal and work situations; 13.3.11.A — Evaluate personal attitudes and work habits that support career retention and advancement.

Essential Questions

How can an organization reset help me finish the week prepared for the next one?

Objectives

  • :
  • Complete the daily Google Form check-in.
  • Review materials and identify unfinished schoolwork that needs attention.
  • Organize a backpack and/or locker using an appropriate system.
  • Participate appropriately in a structured choice activity after required tasks are complete.

Assessment

Formative: teacher observation of chick-in completion, work review, organization behaviors, and student use of an organization system. Product evidence: completed check-in, completed or documented work tasks, and organized backpack/locker. Teacher may use a simple checklist to document independence and level of prompting.

Differentiation

Provide a visual Friday checklist with tasks in order. Allow students to complete organization tasks one area at a time. Provide adult prompting, modeling, and organization support when included in the student's IEP or classroom plan. Offer sorting categories such as 'Keep,' 'Take Home,' 'Turn In,' and 'Needs Attention.' Allow alternative storage systems for students who have difficulty managing a backpack or locker. For enrichment, students can create a personal weekly organization routine or identify one change that would make next week easier.

Classroom Environment & Routines

Use a predictable end-of-week routine: check-in - unfinished work - organization reset - choice activity. Establish clear criteria for moving to games: required academic and organization tasks must be completed or appropriately documented. Maintain a calm, positive environment and reinforce independence rather than speed.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Ask students to think about what happens when papers, assignments, or materials are difficult to find. Connect Friday's organization reset to Monday's self-assessment and the class's ongoing focus on executive functioning. Explain that organization is a habit that can make school and work responsibilities easier to manage

Materials Needed

Daily Google Form, student computers, unfinished assignments/materials, backpacks, lockers if applicable, organization checklist, folders, labels or sorting categories as appropriate, board games, UNO, puzzles, LEGOs, or other classroom-approved choice activities.

Vocabulary

Organization, reset, unfinished work, priority, checklist, routine, materials, sort, organize, independent, responsibility.

Lesson Sequence

  • 0–5 min — Daily Google Form check-in: Students complete the form and review the day's checklist.
  • 5–15 min — Work completion check: Students review assignments, folders, digital work, and teacher directions. Teacher helps students identify what is finished, what is missing, and what needs to be completed or addressed.
  • 15-50 min — Complete/organize: Students complete priority work when possible and/or organize materials needed for upcoming classes. Teacher uses a checklist and prompts students to work independently before providing more direct assistance.
  • 50-55 min — Backpack/locker reset: Students organize their backpack and locker if applicable. Suggested categories include 'Keep at school,' 'Take home,' 'Turn in,' and 'Needs attention.' Teacher checks for a functional system rather than appearance alone.
  • 55-70 min — Structured choice activity: Once required tasks are complete, students may choose from approved games and activities such as UNO, Monopoly, puzzles, LEGOs, or board games. Teacher observes cooperation, communication, and following classroom expectations.
  • 70-75 min — Clean-up and weekly reflection: Students return materials and identify one organization habit they want to use next week.

Closure

Students answer: 'What is one thing I organized today that will make next week easier?' Reinforce that the Friday reset is part of a weekly routine and preview that the class will continue building executive functioning skills the following week.
