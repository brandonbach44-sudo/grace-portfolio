PA Standards

  • Career Education and Work – 13.3.11.F: Demonstrate appropriate work habits and behaviors.
  • Mathematics – PA Academic Standards: Students will apply mathematical concepts and skills to solve problems and interpret mathematical information in meaningful contexts.

Essential Questions

  • Students will consider:
  • How can I use counting skills to solve a problem?
  • How can I compare numbers?
  • How can I follow a checklist to prepare for class?
  • How can I use technology to complete a math activity?
  • How can I practice being independent when using online programs?

Objectives

  • By the end of the lesson, students will:
  • Follow the classroom beginning-of-class checklist with verbal and/or visual prompting as needed.
  • Participate in a five-minute mindfulness activity and review classroom expectations.
  • Demonstrate counting skills during the "What's the Number Wednesday" warm-up.
  • Group 1: Count squares on a graph and communicate the number counted using their AAC devices with teacher support as needed.
  • Group 2: Count squares on a graph and answer comparison questions, including identifying which number is greatest and which is smallest.
  • Access and log into IXL in preparation for the upcoming benchmark.
  • Access and log into Bankability in preparation for the first use of the program during the week.
  • Demonstrate appropriate participation and work behaviors during individual and small-group activities.

Assessment

  • Following the beginning-of-class checklist.
  • Participating in mindfulness and classroom expectations.
  • Responding to the "What's the Number Wednesday" counting activity.
  • Using AAC devices to communicate answers when appropriate.
  • Verbally answering questions during the warm-up.
  • Demonstrating the ability to compare numbers for students working at that level.
  • Successfully logging into IXL.
  • Successfully logging into Bankability.
  • Following directions during transitions between activities.
  • Participating appropriately during small-group instruction.

Differentiation

  • Group 1 – Foundational Skills / Intensive Communication Supports
  • Students in Group 1 use AAC devices to communicate.
  • Teacher uses both verbal language and her own AAC/talking device to model important vocabulary and directions.
  • For example, the teacher may verbally say "Go get your folder" while simultaneously selecting "folder" on her AAC device.
  • Teacher models functional communication such as "hello," "folder," "number," and other relevant vocabulary.
  • Students use their own AAC devices to communicate answers.
  • Counting activity focuses on counting the squares on a graph.
  • Students communicate the number they counted using their AAC devices.
  • Directions are presented in short, clear steps.
  • Students receive additional wait time for communication.
  • Teacher provides prompting as needed and allows students opportunities to respond independently.
  • Visual and verbal supports are provided throughout the lesson.
  • Group 2 – More Independent Learners
  • Students receive the same general directions but require fewer communication supports.
  • Students verbally answer questions.
  • During the counting activity, students count the squares and answer questions such as:
  • "Which number is biggest?"
  • "Which number is smallest?"
  • Students are encouraged to explain their answers when appropriate.
  • Students work toward greater independence when accessing IXL and Bankability.
  • Teacher provides prompts or assistance only when necessary.
  • For Both Groups
  • Students receive access to the same classroom routines.
  • Students receive individualized prompting based on their needs.
  • Students are provided opportunities to demonstrate independence before teacher assistance is given.
  • Expectations are adjusted to students' individual skill levels while maintaining participation in the same lesson.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • The classroom follows a predictable beginning-of-class routine to support organization, independence, and successful transitions.
  • When students enter:
  • Students refer to the checklist displayed on the board.
  • Students gather their required materials.
  • Students prepare their folders/materials for class.
  • Students participate in a five-minute mindfulness meditation.
  • Students review classroom expectations.
  • Students participate in the warm-up.
  • Students transition into small-group activities.
  • A positive reinforcement system is used throughout the lesson. Students earn a fake dollar bill when they answer questions, participate appropriately, or demonstrate positive classroom behaviors.
  • The teacher uses specific positive feedback to reinforce participation and effort.
  • For students who use AAC, the classroom environment supports multiple forms of communication. Students are given adequate time to use their devices and are not expected to respond verbally when AAC is their established communication method.

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • The lesson begins with the familiar classroom checklist. Students are prompted to gather their materials and prepare for class.
  • The teacher then leads a five-minute mindfulness meditation followed by a review of classroom expectations. This provides a predictable transition into academic instruction and prepares students for learning.
  • Students then participate in "What's the Number Wednesday," a familiar counting activity from News-2-You. Using a familiar routine helps activate previously practiced counting skills while allowing the teacher to differentiate the level of questions based on each group's needs.
  • Students are motivated through:
  • Familiar classroom routines
  • Interactive questioning
  • Use of AAC devices
  • Digital learning programs
  • Positive reinforcement using classroom fake dollar bills
  • Opportunities to demonstrate independence

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1 – Approximately 45 minutes
  • 1. Arrival/Beginning-of-Class Routine – 5 minutes
  • Students enter the classroom.
  • Teacher directs students' attention to the checklist on the board.
  • Students gather their folders and other required materials.
  • Teacher provides verbal and visual/AAC support as needed.
  • Teacher models language using her AAC device alongside verbal directions.
  • Example:
  • Teacher says:
  • "Go get your folder."
  • Teacher simultaneously selects "folder" on her AAC device.
  • This provides students with both verbal language and an AAC model.
  • 2. Mindfulness – 5 minutes
  • Students participate in a five-minute mindfulness meditation.
  • Teacher provides reminders and prompts as necessary.
  • Students transition to the academic portion of class.
  • 3. Classroom Expectations – 3–5 minutes
  • Teacher reviews classroom expectations.
  • Students listen and/or respond to questions.
  • Students are reminded of the classroom reinforcement system.
  • Students can earn classroom dollar bills for participation and answering questions.
  • 4. What's the Number Wednesday – Approximately 10 minutes
  • Teacher introduces the News-2-You counting activity.
  • Students count the squares on the graph.
  • Teacher provides prompts as needed.
  • Students use their AAC devices to communicate the number they counted.
  • Teacher models relevant math vocabulary through verbal language and AAC.
  • Students receive positive reinforcement for participation.
  • 5. Small-Group/Digital Learning Activity – Approximately 15 minutes
  • Students are divided into smaller groups.
  • Group A:
  • Students practice logging into IXL in preparation for their upcoming benchmark.
  • Teacher supports students with:
  • Finding the website/program
  • Entering login information
  • Navigating to the appropriate area
  • Troubleshooting login issues
  • Increasing independence with the routine
  • Group B:
  • Students practice logging into Bankability in preparation for using the program later in the week.
  • Teacher supports students with:
  • Accessing the program
  • Logging in
  • Navigating the platform
  • Becoming familiar with the program
  • 6. Groups Switch – Approximately 5 minutes
  • Students transition to the other digital learning activity.
  • Teacher provides transition reminders and prompts.
  • Students practice accessing the second program.
  • 7.Anyone who finishes early can go back and play UNO with TA
  • Group 2 – Approximately 45 minutes
  • The same overall routine and lesson structure is used.
  • 1. Beginning-of-Class Checklist – 5 minutes
  • Students:
  • Check the board.
  • Gather materials.
  • Get their folders.
  • Prepare for class.
  • Teacher encourages greater independence with the routine.
  • 2. Mindfulness – 5 minutes
  • Students participate in the five-minute mindfulness activity.
  • 3. Classroom Expectations – 3–5 minutes
  • Teacher reviews classroom expectations and reminds students about the classroom dollar reinforcement system.
  • 4. What's the Number Wednesday – Approximately 10 minutes
  • Students complete the same News-2-You counting activity.
  • Students:
  • Count the squares on the graph.
  • Identify the number represented.
  • Answer comparison questions such as:
  • "Which number is biggest?"
  • "Which number is smallest?"
  • Teacher asks follow-up questions when appropriate and encourages students to explain their answers.
  • 5. Small-Group/Digital Learning Activities – Approximately 15 minutes
  • Students are divided into smaller groups.
  • One group practices logging into IXL in preparation for the upcoming benchmark.
  • The other group practices logging into Bankability in preparation for using the program later in the week.
  • Teacher monitors students' ability to navigate each program and provides assistance as needed.
  • 6. Groups Switch – Approximately 5 minutes
  • Students switch to the other digital learning activity.
  • Teacher monitors transitions and supports students in accessing the second program.
  • 7.Anyone who finishes early can go back and play UNO with TA

Closure

  • Ask questions such as:
  • "What did we practice today?"
  • "What number did you count?"
  • "Which number was the biggest?"
  • "Which number was the smallest?"
  • "What programs did we practice logging into?"
  • "What did you do when you needed help?"
  • Provide positive feedback for participation and effort.
