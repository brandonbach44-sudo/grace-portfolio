02/11Life Skills Math

PA Standards

  • CC.2.4.3.A.4: Represent and interpret data using tally marks and bar graphs.
  • IEP goals: Students use their AAC device or their voice to communicate, following each student’s IEP goals.

Essential Questions

  • How can tally marks help me count votes?
  • How does a bar graph show which choice got the most votes?
  • How can our votes help us plan our community trip?

Objectives

  • Students will be able to:
  • Group 1: Color in a bar graph to show the number of votes for each shopping place and restaurant.
  • Group 2: Count the votes, make tally marks, and fill in a bar graph showing the votes for each shopping place and restaurant.
  • Both groups: Use the graph to see which choices got the most votes for our community-based instruction (CBI) trip.

Assessment

  • Group 1: Student work coloring in the bar graph.
  • Group 2: Student vote counts, tally marks, and filled-in bar graph.
  • Both groups: Observation of AAC use or verbal responses during the expectations and the video.

Differentiation

  • Content: Students already voted, so the graph uses their own choices.
  • Content: The BrainPOP video on voting and tallies gives students pictures and spoken information.
  • Process: Group 1 colors in the bar graph, and Group 2 counts, tallies, and fills in the graph.
  • AAC: I model my AAC device while giving directions.
  • Process: Adult support is given as each student needs it.
  • IEP/504: Accommodations in each student’s IEP/504 plan will be followed.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Class is shorter today, so we skip the warm-up.
  • Same routine as usual: meditation, then expectations.
  • Both groups watch the BrainPOP video before starting.
  • Students vote on a trip they are about to take, so the work has a real purpose.
  • Adults give help as needed.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Students voted on where they want to go for the upcoming CBI trip to shop for Christmas and eat lunch. Today they use their own votes to make a graph, which connects the math to a real trip they are looking forward to.

Materials Needed

Meditation, classroom expectations, students’ AAC devices, teacher AAC device, BrainPOP video on voting and tallies, vote results for shopping places and restaurants, bar graph, crayons or colored pencils, pencils

Vocabulary

Vote; tally; count; bar graph; most; fewer; shopping; restaurant

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1
  • Meditation.
  • Expectations: I use my AAC device to find them and students use theirs.
  • No warm-up today because class is shorter.
  • We watch the BrainPOP video on voting and tallies.
  • Students color in the bar graph to show the number of votes for each shopping place and restaurant.
  • I model my AAC device throughout.
  • Group 2
  • Meditation.
  • Expectations, said aloud.
  • No warm-up today because class is shorter.
  • We watch the BrainPOP video on voting and tallies.
  • Students count the votes for each shopping place and restaurant.
  • Students make tally marks for each vote.
  • Students fill out the bar graph with the amount of votes.

Closure

  • Group 1: I use my AAC device to ask which place or restaurant has the most votes, using their colored graph.
  • Group 2: I ask students which shopping place and restaurant got the most votes and how they know from the graph.
  • Connection to Next Lesson: Students use the results to plan the CBI trip for Christmas shopping and lunch.
