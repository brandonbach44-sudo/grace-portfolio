Counting Dollars: Do I Have Enough?

B2_9-14LessonPlan.docx

Also in: Block 2 Math/Week 3/Lesson Plans

09/14Money & BankingLife Skills Math

PA Standards

  • PA Academic Standards for Personal Finance, 17.3.9-12.A — Develop a process for making informed spending decisions, considering factors such as price and reliability of information.
  • PA Academic Standards for Personal Finance, 17.3.9-12.C — Develop a personal approach to keeping track of income and spending.
  • PA Academic Standards for Personal Finance, 17.3.9-12.D — Evaluate budgeting approaches and methods for organizing money.
  • Career Education and Work connections: students practice financial decision-making, responsibility, and life-readiness skills.

Essential Questions

  • How can I use money responsibly?
  • How can I tell whether I have enough money to buy something?
  • Why is it important to keep track of money, spending, saving, and bills?

Objectives

  • By the end of the lesson, students will:
  • Count a given amount of money using one-dollar bills with increasing independence.
  • Determine whether they have enough money to purchase a pictured/itemized product by comparing the amount they have with the cost.
  • Identify where money, bills, and spending information are located within Digitability/Bankability.
  • Demonstrate an understanding that money can be spent or saved for a future purchase.
  • Participate using speech, AAC, sign language, pointing, writing, or another established communication method.
  • Demonstrate the targeted skills with at least 80% accuracy, with IEP accommodations and prompting as needed.

Assessment

  • Teacher observation during the money-counting warm-up.
  • Whiteboard responses showing the amount of money each student has.
  • Yes/No responses using AAC, speech, pointing, or another communication method when deciding whether the student has enough money for a product.
  • Teacher questioning during Digitability/Bankability review.
  • Observation of students locating their account balance, bills, shopping area, and saving/spending options.
  • Completion/progress within IXL as an additional individualized math practice measure.

Differentiation

  • Provide AAC devices and model functional vocabulary such as “cost,” “money,” “buy,” “save,” and “bank.”
  • Pair verbal directions with visual directions, gestures, modeling, and the posted visual schedule.
  • Provide clearly pictured products and one-dollar bills for students who benefit from concrete materials.
  • Allow students to point, gesture, use AAC, sign, write, or verbally respond.
  • Reduce the number of choices/items when needed and provide one-step directions.
  • Use least-to-most prompting and fade prompts when students demonstrate independence.
  • Provide adult assistance with counting or writing when required by the student’s IEP.
  • Allow extended time and repeated practice.
  • Use the FM device and preferential positioning as appropriate.
  • Use a calculator or other established math accommodation when appropriate and consistent with the student’s IEP.
  • For students demonstrating mastery, increase the amount of money, introduce multiple items, or ask them to explain whether they should spend or save.
  • IXL and Digitability activities may be individualized to each student’s instructional level.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Students will enter, read the visual schedule, collect their math materials, and sit in their appropriate work area.
  • The teacher will greet each student individually and use the AAC device to model a functional greeting.
  • Students will review classroom expectations at the beginning of each instructional block.
  • The posted visual schedule and timers will communicate transitions and reduce uncertainty.
  • Students will receive specific positive reinforcement for following routines, participating, communicating, and completing tasks.
  • During the class switch, the second group will have a predictable snack and break routine before beginning instruction.
  • Students may use established regulation supports, such as a brief break, appropriate fidgets, or a quiet work location, as needed and consistent with classroom plans.
  • Adults will use calm, concise directions and consistent language.
  • Students will be prompted to return to the task after breaks using the visual schedule/timer.
  • All students will have access to materials and communication supports so that physical or communication needs do not prevent participation.

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • Preparing students for the lesson
  • Begin with the familiar Money Monday routine and connect the lesson to students’ previous experience with Digitability/Bankability.
  • Ask students what money is used for and allow responses through speech, AAC, pointing, signs, or gestures.
  • Show pictured grocery items and ask students to predict whether a given amount of money is enough.
  • Explain that today’s activities practice everyday skills such as shopping, saving money, checking an account, and paying bills.

Materials Needed

Math folders, laptops, whiteboard, whiteboard markers, projection screen, math slide show, visual schedule, AAC device, FM device, timers, fake dollar bills, IXL program

Vocabulary

Bankability, Digitability, Cost, Amount, Budget, Bills, Deposit, Savings, Shop, Spend, Bank

Lesson Sequence

  • 0–5 minutes — Entry-Let the kids read the schedule on the board, grab their math materials and sit in their seats, use the AAC device to say hello to all students individually, reward students with cash when they get their folders
  • 5–12 minutes — Start the morning mindfulness video once all students have their materials and are ready to start. After mindfulness, students will read the expectations. They will raise their hand and share an expectation on their AAC device, pass them reward money as they do this. Use AAC device to help them as needed. Show them the math sign language for the week: “cost.” Demonstrate how to sign “cost” and allow the students to try as well. Play the quick video that also shows the kids how to sign “cost.”
  • 12-22 minutes — Warm -Up: Money Monday- Show a video “Money 101”. After the video finishes, hand students dollar bills to use for the warm-up. They will need their white boards and markers as well. Give one student 5 dollars, one student 3 dollars, one student 4 dollars and one student 2 dollars. Have the student count the money they were given and write it on their white board (or have an adult write it as needed). We will then look at 4 different grocery store products and ask the kids if they have enough for the products. (Yes or No on AAC device)
  • 22–34 minutes — Whole Group Lesson on Digitability: Allow students to login to their digitability accounts. They have all used this program before so it will be a review on where to find their bills/bank account information/ where to shop, etc. We will first look at where they can see how much is in their account and they can see if they have money left over from last year. We will also discuss the bills that they will pay throughout the school year and when they will pay them. Make sure to give the students a reminder that they can save their money or they can use it to stop every week. If they want a bigger prize they will need to save their money until they can afford it.
  • 35–48 minutes — Classes switch, second group comes in and looks at the schedule on the board. They will grab their math materials and sit down for snack time. After they have their snack, they have break time where they can sit anywhere in the room and do any activity of their choice. There will be a timer on the board that tells them when to get back to their seat for class time.
  • 48–55 minutes — Start the morning mindfulness video once all students have their materials and are ready to start. After mindfulness, students will read the expectations. They will raise their hand and share an expectation, pass them reward money as they do this. Show them the math sign language for the week: “cost.” Demonstrate how to sign “cost” and allow the students to try as well. Play the quick video that also shows the kids how to sign “cost.”
  • 55–65 minutes — Warm -Up: Money Monday- Show a video “Money 101”. Explain why _____
  • 65–85 minutes – Allow students to finish their IXL benchmarks, when/if students are finished, they can come to the back table and login to their digitability accounts. They have all used this program before so it will be a review on where to find their bills/bank account information/ where to shop, etc. We will first look at where they can see how much is in their account and they can see if they have money left over from last year. We will also discuss the bills that they will pay throughout the school year and when they will pay them. Make sure to give the students a reminder that they can save their money or they can use it to stop every week. If they want a bigger prize they will need to save their money until they can afford it.
  • 80–90 minutes — Closure/Review: Review digitability/bankability rules/expectations with students and allow them a 5 minute-break for working so hard
  • Closure:
  • Review the students’ Bankability/Digitability expectations and ask each student to identify or demonstrate one money skill from the lesson. Reinforce the importance of knowing how much money is available, understanding the cost of an item, and deciding whether to spend or save. Remind students of the routine for coming to the “bank” and depositing money and review when bills are due. Preview that the next lesson will build on these skills through additional functional money activities.

Closure

questions in which students identify one money skill learned or demonstrate a related routine.
