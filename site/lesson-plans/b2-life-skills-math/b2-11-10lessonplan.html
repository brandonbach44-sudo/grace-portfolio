02/11Life Skills Math

PA Standards

  • CC.2.4.2.A.3: Tell and show time on a clock.
  • CC.2.4.3.A.2: Solve problems involving intervals of time (elapsed time).
  • IEP goals: Students use their AAC device or their voice to communicate, following each student’s IEP goals.

Essential Questions

  • How do I show a time on a clock?
  • How do I find how much time has passed (elapsed time)?
  • How can I show what I know on my own?

Objectives

  • Students will be able to:
  • Group 1: Show the time that is on the board using their clock.
  • Group 2: Find elapsed time using their Judy clocks.
  • Both groups: Complete their individual progress monitoring independently.

Assessment

  • Group 1: Student clock shows during the warm-up.
  • Group 2: Student answers on elapsed time with their Judy clocks.
  • Both groups: Individual progress monitoring, completed independently. The adult with each student takes data.
  • Both groups: Observation of AAC use or verbal responses during the daily routine.

Differentiation

  • Group 1: I use a clock to model what each student’s clock should look like.
  • Group 2: Judy clocks give students a hands-on way to find elapsed time.
  • Both groups: Each student has a teacher, PCA, or teaching assistant with them during progress monitoring.
  • Both groups: Students complete progress monitoring independently while the adult takes data.
  • IEP/504: Accommodations in each student’s IEP/504 plan will be followed.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Same daily routine for both groups: meditation, expectations, then the Time Tuesday warm-up.
  • Time Tuesday is a familiar weekly routine.
  • Each student works with a teacher, PCA, or teaching assistant during progress monitoring.
  • Students work independently, and the adult takes data.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Students use the same daily routine and the weekly Time Tuesday warm-up. The warm-up gets students thinking about time before they work independently on progress monitoring.

Materials Needed

Meditation, classroom expectations, students’ AAC devices, teacher AAC device, clock for modeling, student clocks, Judy clocks, times on the board, elapsed time questions, individual progress monitoring for each student, pencils

Vocabulary

Clock; time; hour; minute; hour hand; minute hand; elapsed time; start; end

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1
  • Meditation.
  • Expectations: I use my AAC device to find them and students use theirs.
  • Time Tuesday warm-up: students show me the time that is on the board.
  • I use a clock to model what each student’s clock should look like.
  • Progress monitoring: each student works independently with a teacher, PCA, or teaching assistant, who takes data.
  • Group 2
  • Meditation.
  • Expectations, said aloud.
  • Time Tuesday warm-up: students find elapsed time using their Judy clocks.
  • Progress monitoring: each student works independently with a teacher, PCA, or teaching assistant, who takes data.

Closure

  • Group 1: I use my AAC device to let students know they did a great job, and ask them to share one time they showed on their clock.
  • Group 2: I ask students to share how they found elapsed time.
  • Connection to Next Lesson: Students continue practicing telling time and elapsed time, and I use the progress monitoring data to plan next steps.
