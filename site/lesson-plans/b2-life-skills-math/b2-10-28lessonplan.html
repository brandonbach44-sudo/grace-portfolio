10/28Counting, Number Sense & DataLife Skills Math

PA Standards

  • CC.2.4.3.A.4: Represent and interpret data using scaled pictographs and bar graphs.
  • CC.2.4.HS.B.1: Summarize and interpret data, including finding the mean, median, and mode.
  • IEP goals: Students use their AAC device or their voice to communicate, following each student’s IEP goals.

Essential Questions

  • What can a graph tell me?
  • How can I compare groups on a bar graph or pictograph to find which has more?
  • How do the mean, median, and mode describe a set of numbers?

Objectives

  • Students will be able to:
  • Group 1: Read a bar graph to tell which color is higher, and read a News2you pictograph to find which picture appears most or count the pictures, based on their IEP goal.
  • Group 2: Read a bar graph to tell which category got the most votes, compare categories, and name the most popular device. Then find the mean, median, and mode of data shown on graphs.
  • Both groups: Follow the daily routine of mindfulness, expectations, and the ASL sign of the week.

Assessment

  • Group 1: Student responses to “What color is higher?” (by AAC device, pointing, or voice) and to the pictograph task, matched to each IEP goal.
  • Group 2: Student responses about votes, comparing categories, and the most popular device, followed by the mean, median, and mode answers they find.
  • Both groups: Observation of AAC use or verbal responses during the daily routine.

Differentiation

  • Group 1: I ask a simpler question (which color is higher) and students pick which picture appears most or count, whichever is closest to their IEP goal.
  • Group 1: I model my AAC device while asking questions.
  • Group 2: I ask more in-depth questions: compare categories and name the most popular device, then find mean, median, and mode.
  • Both groups: The same graph is used for both groups, so everyone works on the same idea at a different level.
  • Reinforcement: Fake money is earned for participating and helping a friend.
  • IEP/504: Accommodations in each student’s IEP/504 plan will be followed.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Same daily routine every day: materials, mindfulness, expectations, and ASL sign of the week, so students know what to expect.
  • What’s the Number Wednesday is a familiar weekly routine.
  • Group 1 hears and sees questions at a level that fits their IEP goal. I model the AAC device while I ask.
  • Group 2 takes a snack/leisure break first, with a timer on the board.
  • Students earn fake money for participating and helping a friend.

Activating Prior Knowledge

The daily routines (mindfulness, expectations, and the ASL sign of the week) and the weekly What’s the Number Wednesday graph are familiar to students. The topic, “What is your favorite technology?”, connects to students’ own interests and helps them stay engaged.

Materials Needed

Visual schedule, projector/board, mindfulness video, students’ AAC devices, teacher AAC device, classroom expectations, ASL sign of the week, What’s the Number Wednesday bar graph (“What’s your favorite technology?”), News2you pictograph, graphs for mean/median/mode, pencils, fake money, timer

Vocabulary

Graph; bar graph; pictograph; most; compare; votes; popular; mean; median; mode

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1 — 9:15–9:52
  • Students gather their materials.
  • Daily mindfulness.
  • Review the expectations: I use my AAC device to find them and students use theirs.
  • ASL sign of the week.
  • What’s the Number Wednesday: bar graph with two colors, “What’s your favorite technology?” I ask, “What color is higher?”
  • News2you pictograph lesson: students choose which picture appears most, or count the pictures, whichever is closest to their IEP goal.
  • I model my AAC device throughout and hand out fake money for participating and helping a friend.
  • Group 2 — begins at 9:52
  • Students grab their materials.
  • Snack and leisure break until 10:08 (timer on the board).
  • Daily meditation.
  • Review the expectations aloud.
  • ASL sign of the week.
  • What’s the Number Wednesday: the same bar graph, with more in-depth questions. Students tell me what got more votes, compare categories, and answer what the most popular device is.
  • Students look at graphs and find the mean, median, and mode.
  • I hand out fake money for participating and helping a friend.

Closure

  • Group 1: I use my AAC device to ask students to share one thing they learned from the graphs, such as which color or picture was most.
  • Group 2: I ask students to tell me the mean, median, and mode they found and what the graph tells us about favorite technology.
  • Connection to Next Lesson: Students continue reading graphs and using data in everyday situations.
