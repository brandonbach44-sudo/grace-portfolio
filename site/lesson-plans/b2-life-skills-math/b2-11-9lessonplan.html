02/11Life Skills Math

PA Standards

  • Functional money skills: Count money (dollar bills and coins) and find change, and decide whether there is enough money for an item.
  • IEP goals: Students use their AAC device or their voice to communicate, following each student’s IEP goals.

Essential Questions

  • How do I count money?
  • How do I find the change I will get back?
  • How do I know if I have enough money to buy something?

Objectives

  • Students will be able to:
  • Group 1: Count out a set number of dollar bills and count coins, coming up to the board to show their work, using their AAC device or voice.
  • Group 2: Count money, find the change they will get for items with set prices, and decide whether they have enough money for certain things.
  • Both groups: Use the ASL sign of the week, “buy.”

Assessment

  • Group 1: Student responses counting out dollar bills and coins, including at the board.
  • Group 2: Student answers counting money and finding change, and deciding if they have enough.
  • Both groups: Observation of AAC use or verbal responses during the daily routine.

Differentiation

  • Group 1: We use AAC devices with this group, and I model my AAC device.
  • Group 1: Students come up to the board to count coins.
  • Group 2: The News2you lesson is more in-depth, with change and deciding if there is enough money.
  • Group 2: Group 2 takes a break and snack first.
  • Both groups: Adult support is given as each student needs it.
  • IEP/504: Accommodations in each student’s IEP/504 plan will be followed.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Same daily routine for both groups: meditation, expectations, warm-up, and ASL sign of the week.
  • Money Monday is a familiar weekly routine.
  • Group 1 uses AAC devices, and I model mine.
  • Group 2 takes a break and snack first.
  • Group 2’s lesson is more in-depth than Group 1’s.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Students use the daily routine and the weekly Money Monday warm-up. The ASL sign “buy” connects to the money they count. The lesson is similar to the warm-up, so students practice the same skill in a new way.

Materials Needed

Meditation, classroom expectations, students’ AAC devices, teacher AAC device, dollar bills, coins, board, News2you money lesson, items with set prices, snack items, pencils

Vocabulary

Buy; money; dollar; coin; count; change; price; enough

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1
  • Meditation.
  • Expectations: I use my AAC device to find them and students use theirs.
  • Money Monday warm-up: students count out a set number of dollar bills.
  • ASL sign of the week, “buy”: I demonstrate and students try it.
  • Money lesson, similar to the warm-up: students count coins and come up to the board to do it.
  • We use AAC devices throughout.
  • Group 2
  • Break and snack.
  • Meditation.
  • Expectations, said aloud.
  • Money Monday warm-up: students count money and find the change they will get for items with set prices.
  • ASL sign of the week, “buy”: I demonstrate and students try it.
  • News2you money lesson, more in-depth than Group 1: students count money, find change, and decide whether they have enough for certain things.

Closure

  • Group 1: I use my AAC device to ask students to share one thing they counted, such as dollar bills or coins.
  • Group 2: I ask students to tell me how they found their change and how they knew if they had enough.
  • Connection to Next Lesson: Students continue practicing counting money and making change.
