Telling Time & Reading Pictographs

10/06Time & Measurement · Counting, Number Sense & DataLife Skills Math

PA Standards

This lesson addresses Pennsylvania mathematics standards through functional application of mathematical skills, including telling time, interpreting data presented in graphs, comparing quantities, addition, and using money. The lesson also incorporates functional communication, independence, and transition-related skills.

Essential Questions

  •  How can I use a clock to understand and communicate time?
  •  How can I use a pictograph to understand information?
  •  How can I use math skills in real-life situations?

Objectives

  •  Demonstrate understanding of time by modeling a given time on a Judy clock.
  •  Identify the hour and minute hands on an analog clock.
  •  Answer questions about time based on their individual instructional level.
  •  Interpret information presented in a pictograph by counting, comparing, and identifying quantities.
  •  Communicate mathematical answers using AAC devices, verbal responses, pointing, or other established communication methods as appropriate.
  •  Group 2 students will practice determining what a clock will look like after 30 minutes have passed.
  •  Students completing IXL will work on their individualized recommended math skills.
  •  The student completing the time-card activity will practice recording time worked and calculating money earned as a functional workplace/office skill.

Assessment

  • Students will show what they know by:
  • Participating in the classroom routines and expectations.
  • Responding to teacher prompts using AAC devices or verbal communication.
  • Demonstrating the ASL sign of the week, "deposit," when able.
  • Modeling times on Judy clocks.
  • Identifying the hour and minute hands.
  • Answering questions about what happens when 30 minutes pass.
  • Answering questions based on pictographs.
  • Completing individualized IXL recommendations.
  • Completing the time-card and money calculation activity.
  • Teacher observation, questioning, prompting, and review of student work will be used as ongoing formative assessment.

Differentiation

  • Group 1:
  • Students will receive AAC modeling throughout the lesson. The teacher will use an AAC device to greet students, give directions, model vocabulary, and communicate when appropriate. Students will receive additional processing time, repeated directions, visual supports, modeling, prompting, and hands-on materials such as Judy clocks. Pictograph questions will be adjusted to each student's individual instructional level. Students may answer through their AAC device, verbally, by pointing, or through another established communication method.
  • Group 2:
  • Students will receive more opportunities for independent work and higher-level questioning. Students will practice telling time to the half hour and reasoning about elapsed time. Students will work on individualized IXL recommendations based on their needs.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • A visual schedule will be projected on the whiteboard when students enter the classroom. The schedule will identify the activities for the day as well as the materials students will need. Students will be prompted as needed to gather their materials.
  • The classroom follows a consistent routine to help students understand expectations and transitions. Group 1 will begin with materials, mindfulness, classroom expectations, the ASL sign of the week, the Time Tuesday warm-up, and the day's math activity.
  • Group 1 students will receive AAC modeling throughout the lesson. The teacher will model appropriate AAC use while also providing students with opportunities to use their own devices.
  • Group 2 will begin with snack and a 10-minute leisure break before beginning the instructional routine.
  • Fake money will be provided throughout the warm-up as positive reinforcement for participation and following classroom expectations. The teacher will use positive reinforcement, prompting, visual supports, redirection, wait time, and individual assistance to support student engagement and independence.

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • The projected visual schedule will prepare students for the activities they will complete during class. The consistent classroom routine will help students anticipate the sequence of activities.
  • The Time Tuesday warm-up will activate students' previous knowledge of telling time and using an analog clock. Students will use hands-on Judy clocks to physically model different times.
  • For Group 1, the teacher will model time on a Judy clock and students will copy the time on their own clocks. Students will then identify which picture matches the time shown.
  • For Group 2, students will independently demonstrate a time on their Judy clocks and identify the hour and minute hands. The teacher will extend the activity by asking what the clock would look like if 30 minutes passed.
  • The use of fake money during participation provides additional motivation and reinforcement.

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1 — 45 Minutes
  • 1. Entry Routine and Materials
  • Approx. 0–5 minutes
  • The visual schedule and materials needed for the day will be projected on the whiteboard as students enter.
  • Students will be prompted to gather their materials.
  • Teacher will greet students using the AAC device.
  • Teacher will use the AAC device to tell students to get their materials.
  • AAC modeling will continue throughout the lesson when appropriate.
  • 2. Mindfulness
  • Approx. 5–10 minutes
  • Students will participate in a mindfulness meditation video.
  • Teacher will provide prompts as needed to help students attend to the activity.
  • 3. Classroom Expectations
  • Approx. 10–15 minutes
  • Teacher will review the classroom expectations.
  • Students will have the opportunity to communicate the expectations using their AAC devices.
  • Teacher will provide assistance, modeling, and wait time as needed.
  • Students who are able may verbally state expectations.
  • 4. ASL Sign of the Week
  • Approx. 15–18 minutes
  • Teacher will introduce the ASL sign of the week: "deposit."
  • Teacher will model the sign.
  • Students will attempt the sign if they are able.
  • 5. Time Tuesday Warm-Up
  • Approx. 18–28 minutes
  • Teacher will model a time using the teacher Judy clock.
  • Students will use their own Judy clocks to model the same time.
  • Teacher will display pictures showing different times.
  • Students will identify which picture matches the time on their clock.
  • Students may answer using AAC devices, speech, pointing, or another established response method.
  • Teacher will provide fake money as students participate and follow classroom expectations.
  • Teacher will provide additional prompts and modeling as needed.
  • 6. Pictograph Practice
  • Approx. 28–45 minutes
  • Teacher will introduce a pictograph.
  • Students will examine the pictures and information shown on the graph.
  • Questions will be differentiated according to individual student ability.
  • Some students may:
  • Count the pictures.
  • Identify how many items are represented.
  • Identify which group has more.
  • Identify which group has less.
  • Compare two quantities.
  • Answer simple questions about the graph.
  • Students will use their AAC devices to communicate answers when appropriate.
  • Teacher will model relevant vocabulary using AAC.
  • Teacher will provide wait time, prompts, and feedback.
  • Teacher will monitor individual student understanding throughout the activity.
  • Group 2 — 45 Minutes
  • 1. Snack and Leisure Break
  • Approx. 0–10 minutes
  • Students will enter and have their snack.
  • Students will participate in a 10-minute leisure break.
  • Students in this group are generally more independent and do not use AAC devices as their primary communication system.
  • 2. Mindfulness
  • Approx. 10–15 minutes
  • Students will participate in the mindfulness meditation video.
  • Teacher will provide reminders and prompts as needed.
  • 3. Classroom Expectations
  • Approx. 15–20 minutes
  • Teacher will review the classroom expectations.
  • Students will identify or discuss the expectations.
  • Teacher will reinforce appropriate participation and classroom behavior.
  • 4. ASL Sign of the Week
  • Approx. 20–23 minutes
  • Teacher will introduce and model the ASL sign "deposit."
  • Students will practice the sign if they are able.
  • 5. Time Tuesday Warm-Up
  • Approx. 23–33 minutes
  • Teacher will display a time on the board.
  • Students will read the time and demonstrate it on their Judy clocks.
  • Students will identify what the hour hand is pointing to.
  • Students will identify what the minute hand is pointing to.
  • Teacher will ask questions such as:
  • "What time is it?"
  • "Which hand is the hour hand?"
  • "Which hand is the minute hand?"
  • "What would it look like if 30 minutes passed?"
  • Students will adjust their clocks to demonstrate the new time.
  • Teacher will check each student's understanding and provide feedback.
  • 6. Individualized Math Practice
  • Approx. 33–45 minutes
  • Students completing IXL:
  • Students will log into IXL.
  • Students will work on their individualized recommended skills.
  • Teacher will circulate and provide assistance.
  • Teacher will monitor student progress and provide prompts or reteaching when needed.
  • Student completing the time-card activity:
  • This student will work with classmates' time cards.
  • Student will determine how much time each classmate worked.
  • Student will add the amount of time worked.
  • Student will calculate how much money the classmates earned.
  • Teacher will provide prompts and check calculations.
  • This activity will provide practice with:
  • Time
  • Addition
  • Money
  • Record keeping
  • Following a workplace procedure
  • Office-related skills
  • This activity is connected to the student's goal of eventually working in an office environment and provides a meaningful opportunity to practice a functional workplace task.

Closure

  • At the end of each 45-minute group, the teacher will briefly review the main skill practiced during the lesson.
  • Group 1:
  • Students will identify or demonstrate something they practiced with time or pictographs.
  • Students may communicate their response using AAC, speech, pointing, or another established method.
  • Group 2:
  • Students will demonstrate a time on their Judy clock or answer a question about what happens when 30 minutes pass.
  • Students may also briefly discuss what they practiced on IXL or during the time-card activity.
  • Teacher will reinforce participation, communication, and independence and will use the visual schedule to help students transition to the next activity or class.
