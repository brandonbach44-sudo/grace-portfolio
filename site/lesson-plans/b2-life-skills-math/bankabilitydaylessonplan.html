Bankability Day (recurring routine)

BankabilityDayLessonPlan.docx

RecurringMoney & BankingLife Skills Math

PA Standards

  • PA Academic Standards for Personal Finance, 17.3.9-12.A — Develop a process for making informed spending decisions.
  • PA Academic Standards for Personal Finance, 17.3.9-12.C — Develop a personal approach to keeping track of income and spending.
  • PA Academic Standards for Personal Finance, 17.3.9-12.D — Evaluate budgeting approaches and methods for organizing money.
  • Career Education and Work, 13.3.11.F — Demonstrate appropriate work habits and behaviors (financial accountability, following routines).
  • Standards for Mathematical Practice: MP1 — Make sense of problems and persevere; MP6 — Attend to precision.

Essential Questions

  • How can I keep track of the money I earn?
  • How do I make a deposit and what does it mean to save money in a bank account?
  • How can I tell whether I have enough money to buy something I want?
  • Why is it important to be responsible with money?

Objectives

  • By the end of the lesson, students will:
  • Count earned cash (one-dollar bills and/or coins) with increasing independence or with appropriate adult support.
  • Complete or participate in completing a deposit slip by recording the correct dollar amount.
  • Navigate the Bankability program to locate their account balance, deposit earnings, and view any bills due.
  • Demonstrate understanding that money can be deposited, saved, or used to purchase items from the school store.
  • Communicate during the banking routine using their established method (AAC device, speech, pointing, gestures, or writing).
  • Demonstrate targeted skills with at least 80% accuracy with IEP accommodations and prompting as needed.

Assessment

  • Students will show what they know by:
  • Teacher observation of counting accuracy during the individual banking rotation.
  • Review of completed or partially completed deposit slips (with assistance as needed).
  • AAC device use, pointing, gestures, or verbal responses during banking and shopping decisions.
  • Teacher data on prompting level needed for each student during the banking routine.
  • Observation of student navigation of Bankability (locating balance, completing deposit).
  • Group 2: calculator use and self-checking when determining whether they can afford items.

Differentiation

  • Modifications for IEPs/504s; Extensions for advanced learners:
  • GROUP 1 (lower level / primarily nonverbal):
  • Model all communication using classroom AAC device; accept any modality (AAC, gesture, pointing, eye gaze, sign).
  • Provide physical prompting, hand-over-hand support, or adult scribing for deposit slips.
  • Use least-to-most prompting; fade when student shows independence.
  • Pair verbal directions with visual supports, gestures, and modeling.
  • TA, PCAs, and cooperating teacher provide 1:1 support during banking rotation.
  • Reduce money denominations or total amount based on individual skill level.
  • GROUP 2 (more independent / verbal):
  • Students count money and complete deposit slips independently before coming to the bank.
  • Students use calculators to add item costs and determine affordability.
  • Extend: ask students to calculate change or compare prices of multiple items.
  • Students verbally explain their banking decisions (deposit, save, or spend).
  • BOTH GROUPS:
  • Visual schedules and timers communicate transitions.
  • Positive reinforcement for following routines and participating.
  • Extended time as needed per IEP.
  • FM device and preferential seating as appropriate per IEP.
  • — Behavior Management Plan & Routines
  • Students enter, locate their math folder and deposit slip at their workstation, and follow the posted visual schedule.
  • Student teacher greets Group 1 individually using the AAC device to model "hello"; Group 2 receives a verbal greeting.
  • Classroom expectations are reviewed at the start of each block; banking routine is previewed.
  • A visual schedule and countdown timer are posted and referenced throughout both blocks.
  • Cooperating teacher, TA, and PCAs circulate during the banking rotation to minimize wait time for Group 1.
  • Positive verbal and tangible reinforcement (reward cash) is provided for following routines and communicating.
  • Group 1: Classroom is arranged for wheelchair/mobility access; AAC devices are charged and available at each workstation.
  • Group 2: Students have a 10-minute snack/leisure break upon arrival before independent work begins.
  • Cool Math Games serves as a structured independent activity after banking is complete.
  • All adults use calm, consistent language and prompting strategies aligned with each student's IEP.

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • How will you activate prior knowledge, build background, or review previous lessons?
  • Students have participated in Bankability throughout the school year and are familiar with earning and depositing money.
  • They have practiced counting dollar bills in previous Life Skills Math lessons.
  • Prior lessons have introduced vocabulary: "deposit," "bank account," "balance," and "bills."
  • Activation for Group 1: Using the AAC device and visuals, ask "What do we do on Bankability day?" during the greeting routine.
  • Activation for Group 2: Brief verbal exchange during entry — "What do we do on Bankability day?" and "What does it mean to deposit money?"

Materials Needed

  • Math folders (one per student)
  • Deposit slips (one per student)
  • Earned cash (one-dollar bills and/or coins collected throughout the week)
  • Bankability computer program (student laptops + teacher computer)
  • AAC devices (Group 1)
  • Calculators (Group 2)
  • Visual schedule and countdown timer
  • School store items / prize menu
  • Cool Math Games (on student laptops)
  • FM device (as applicable per IEP)
  • Reinforcement reward cash

Vocabulary

Deposit, Withdrawal, Balance, Bank Account, Paycheck, Bills, Budget, Save, Spend, Cost, Afford, Receipt

Lesson Sequence

  • Learning activities/strategies, student application, monitoring of performance:
  • ── GROUP 1 (approx. 0–45 min) ──
  • 0–5 min | Entry & Setup
  • Students enter and are assisted by TA/PCAs in locating their math folder and a deposit slip. Student teacher uses the AAC device to individually model "hello" to each student, accepting any communicative response. Visual schedule is reviewed.
  • 5–10 min | Count Money & Fill Out Deposit Slip
  • Each student (with adult support as needed) counts earned cash using least-to-most prompting. Students write or have an adult scribe the total on their deposit slip. AAC device models functional vocabulary: "count," "money," "deposit," "how much."
  • 10–40 min | Individual Banking Rotation
  • Student teacher calls students to the bank (teacher desk/computer) one at a time. At the bank:
  • Student teacher and student count the money together to verify the total.
  • Student teacher enters the deposit into Bankability and adds the weekly $100 paycheck.
  • Student teacher tells the student their new balance and whether any bills are due.
  • If the student has enough money after bills, they may shop at the school store, communicating their choice via AAC, pointing, or gesture.
  • While waiting, students are supported by TA/PCAs with deposit slips, quiet tasks, or regulation supports. Cool Math Games is available as a structured wait activity.
  • 40–45 min | Closure & Transition (Group 1)
  • Student teacher briefly reviews: "Today we made a deposit and checked our balance." Each student is prompted to indicate one banking action (via AAC, gesture, or verbal). Students earn reward cash for participation. Group 1 transitions out.
  • ── TRANSITION (approx. 45–55 min) ──
  • Group 2 enters and locates their math folder and a deposit slip. Students begin a 10-minute snack and leisure break. Timer is posted. Student teacher and TA reset the banking station.
  • ── GROUP 2 (approx. 55–90 min) ──
  • 55–65 min | Independent Money Counting & Deposit Slip Completion
  • After the break timer ends, students sit at workstations. Student teacher briefly reviews: "What are we doing today?" Students independently count earned cash and complete their deposit slip (name, date, amount). Student teacher circulates to check accuracy.
  • 65–85 min | Individual Banking Rotation
  • Student teacher calls students to the bank one at a time. At the bank:
  • Student states: "I would like to make a deposit."
  • Student teacher and student count the money together to verify the deposit slip amount.
  • Student teacher enters the deposit and weekly $100 paycheck into Bankability.
  • Student teacher informs the student of their new balance and any bills due.
  • If the student wishes to shop, they use a calculator to add item costs and confirm affordability before purchasing.
  • Students not yet at the bank work independently: finishing deposit slips, calculating potential purchases from the school store price list, or playing Cool Math Games.
  • 85–90 min | Closure (Group 2)
  • Student teacher brings group together. Students share one thing they did in Bankability today (e.g., "I made a deposit," "I paid a bill," "I saved my money"). Student teacher previews next Bankability day and reminds students to keep earning cash throughout the week.
  • Closure:
  • How will you bring closure to the lesson and preview what follows?
  • Group 1: Student teacher asks each student (via AAC, gesture, or verbal prompt) to indicate one banking action they completed. Reward cash given for participation.
  • Group 2: Students verbally share one thing they did in Bankability. Financial vocabulary is reinforced.
  • Both groups: Student teacher previews the next lesson, students will continue earning cash and have another opportunity to deposit and shop on the next Bankability day.
  • Students are reminded to keep track of their earnings during the week.

Closure

check: each student identifies or demonstrates one banking skill from the lesson.
