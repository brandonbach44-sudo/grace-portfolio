02/11Life Skills Math

PA Standards

  • CC.2.4.3.A.4: Represent and interpret data using tally marks and bar graphs.
  • CC.2.1.3.C.1: Understand fractions as parts of a whole.
  • IEP goals: Students use their AAC device or their voice to communicate, following each student’s IEP goals.

Essential Questions

  • How do tally marks show how many votes each restaurant got?
  • How does a bar graph show which restaurant got the most and the fewest votes?
  • How can I show how much of a whole is gone as a fraction?

Objectives

  • Students will be able to:
  • Group 1: Fill in the number of boxes on the graph that matches the tallies for each restaurant.
  • Group 2: Name a fraction to show how much of a pizza is gone, count the tallies, graph the restaurant votes, and answer questions about which restaurant has the most and the least.
  • Both groups: Use the graph to help plan the CBI trip.

Assessment

  • Group 1: Student graph: the boxes filled in to match the tallies.
  • Group 2: Student fractions for the pizza picture.
  • Group 2: Student tallies, graph, and answers to the follow-up questions.
  • Group 1: Progress monitoring and Bankability prep for the students who work on those while others are at speech.

Differentiation

  • Group 1: Students continue the graph they started, so they can pick up where they left off.
  • Group 1: One student works on progress monitoring and one on Bankability prep until the others return from speech.
  • Group 1: I model my AAC device while giving directions.
  • Group 2: A picture of a pizza gives students a visual for the fraction warm-up.
  • Group 2: Group 2 takes a break and snack first.
  • Both groups: Adult support is given as each student needs it.
  • IEP/504: Accommodations in each student’s IEP/504 plan will be followed.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Class is shorter today, so Group 1 skips the warm-up.
  • Same routine for both groups: meditation, then expectations.
  • Group 1 students work on different tasks until all students are back from speech.
  • Group 2 takes a break and snack, then does a quick fractions warm-up.
  • The restaurant vote graph helps students plan their CBI trip.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Students voted on where they want to eat for the CBI trip and made tallies. Today they use their own tallies to finish the graph, which connects the math to a trip they are looking forward to.

Materials Needed

Meditation, classroom expectations, students’ AAC devices, teacher AAC device, restaurant vote tallies, bar graph, pizza picture, snack items, individual progress monitoring, Bankability prep materials, pencils and crayons

Vocabulary

Tally; vote; graph; most; least; fraction; whole; part; restaurant

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1
  • Meditation.
  • Expectations: I use my AAC device to find them and students use theirs.
  • No warm-up today because class is shorter.
  • One student does progress monitoring and one student does their Bankability prep while the other students are at speech.
  • When the other two students return from speech, we continue graphing our tallies for the restaurant vote for the CBI.
  • Students look at the tallies and fill in the number of boxes on the graph for each restaurant.
  • Group 2
  • Meditation.
  • Expectations, said aloud.
  • Break and snack.
  • Quick fractions warm-up: students look at a picture of a pizza and tell me how much of the pizza is gone, as a fraction.
  • Students count the tallies and graph the restaurant votes.
  • Students answer follow-up questions, such as which restaurant has the most and which has the least.

Closure

  • Group 1: I use my AAC device to ask students which restaurant has the most boxes on their graph.
  • Group 2: I ask students to share which restaurant has the most and which has the least, and how they know from the graph.
  • Connection to Next Lesson: Students use the results to plan the CBI trip.
