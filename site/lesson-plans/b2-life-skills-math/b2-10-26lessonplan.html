10/26Time & MeasurementLife Skills Math

PA Standards

  • CC.2.4.3.A.2: Solve problems involving measurement of liquid volumes (measuring amounts in cups).
  • IEP goals: Students use their AAC device or their voice to communicate, following each student’s IEP communication and functional skills goals.

Essential Questions

  • How can I use my AAC device, my voice, or a sign to share ideas and follow classroom expectations?
  • How do I show an amount on a measuring cup?
  • How can I show what I know and ask for help or a break when I need one?

Objectives

  • Students will be able to:
  • Group 1: Find and share the classroom expectations using their AAC device, with modeling from the teacher.
  • Group 2: State the classroom expectations aloud.
  • Both groups: Imitate the ASL sign for “pay” and work on their individual progress monitoring goals with one adult.
  • Group 2: Color in a given amount in cups on a measuring cup to practice for cooking the next day.

Assessment

  • Both groups: Individual progress monitoring for each student, with one adult working with each student.
  • Both groups: Observation of AAC use while finding and sharing expectations, and of attempts at the ASL sign.
  • Group 2: Measurement Monday worksheet (cups colored in on the measuring cup).

Differentiation

  • AAC: I model my AAC device throughout class and students use theirs to find and share expectations.
  • Supports: Visual schedule shows what students need and when Group 1 leaves for speech.
  • Supports: One adult per student during progress monitoring.
  • Supports: A timer on the board for the snack/leisure break.
  • Reinforcement: Fake money earned for getting materials, participating, and helping a friend.
  • Supports: Early finishers in Group 2 may take a break.
  • IEP/504: Accommodations in each student’s IEP/504 plan will be followed.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • A visual schedule on the board shows what students need and when Group 1 leaves for speech (9:40).
  • A timer on the board shows when the snack/leisure break ends for Group 2 (10:08).
  • The same routine each day: materials, mindfulness, expectations, ASL sign of the week, then progress monitoring.
  • Students earn fake money for getting materials, participating, and helping a friend, and shop with it at the end of the week.
  • One adult works with each student during progress monitoring.
  • Group 1 cleans up early so students can get to speech on time. Group 2 early finishers may take a break.

Activating Prior Knowledge

Familiar daily routines (visual schedule, mindfulness, expectations, and the ASL sign of the week) help students know what to expect. This week’s sign, “pay,” connects to the fake money students earn and use to shop at the end of the week. The Measurement Monday worksheet is practice and a warm-up for cooking in class the next day.

Materials Needed

Visual schedule, projector/board, 3-minute mindfulness video, students’ AAC devices, teacher AAC device, classroom expectations, ASL sign of the week (“pay”), fake money, individual progress monitoring for each student, Measurement Monday worksheet, coloring supplies, pencils, timer, snack and leisure items

Vocabulary

Pay; money; cup; measure; amount; expectations; break

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1 — 9:15–9:52 (leaves for speech at 9:40)
  • Visual schedule on the board shows what students need and when they leave for speech.
  • Students gather their materials.
  • 3-minute mindfulness video.
  • Expectations: I use my AAC device to find the classroom expectations and students use theirs to find and share them.
  • ASL sign of the week, “pay”: I demonstrate and students try it.
  • Progress monitoring, with one adult per student. There is no math warm-up because of speech.
  • Students clean up early and go to speech at 9:40.
  • I model my AAC device throughout the session and hand out fake money for getting materials, participating, and helping a friend.
  • Group 2 — begins at 9:52
  • Students grab their materials.
  • Snack and leisure break until 10:08 (timer on the board).
  • Meditation.
  • Expectations, said aloud together.
  • ASL sign of the week, “pay”: I demonstrate and students try it.
  • Measurement Monday worksheet: students color in a given amount in cups on a measuring cup. This is practice and a warm-up for cooking the next day.
  • Progress monitoring, with one adult per student, until the end of class.
  • Students who finish early may take a break.
  • Fake money is handed out for getting materials, participating, and helping a friend.

Closure

  • Group 1: As students clean up, I use my AAC device to let them know it is time for speech and to ask them to share one thing they did today, such as finding the expectations or signing “pay.”
  • Group 2: I review the measuring cup worksheet and ask students to say or show one thing they practiced.
  • Connection to Next Lesson: Students cook in class tomorrow, using the measuring practice from today. We continue modeling AAC use and ASL signs, and students use their fake money to shop at the end of the week.
