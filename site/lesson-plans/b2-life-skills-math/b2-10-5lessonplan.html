10/05Money & BankingLife Skills Math

PA Standards

Pennsylvania Academic Standards for Mathematics, Numbers and Operations: applying number concepts and operations in real-world contexts. This lesson supports functional mathematics, financial literacy, communication, and independent-living skills.

Essential Questions

How much are these coins worth? How can I use money to complete everyday financial tasks? How can I communicate what I know about money?

Objectives

  • Students will know how to tell the value of coins
  • Students will know how to properly fill out a check
  • Students will show they can work independently through progress monitoring

Assessment

  • Group 1: progress monitoring data
  • Group 2: completed money word problems/filled out check
  • Group 2: progress monitoring data
  • Observation of following the daily routine and expectations

Differentiation

  • Group 1: AAC device vocabulary (yes/no, coin value) modeled by the teacher before independent use
  • Group 1: mindfulness video and class expectations modeled using the AAC device to reinforce communication
  • Group 1: cwarm-up coin counting slides use pictures, with low, simple value
  • Group 2: task is more complex, recall information from last weeks lesson, matched to their higher independence level
  • Group 2: visible timer displayed during snack/break to support predictability
  • Visual schedule and materials list posted for both groups
  • Progress monitoring tailored to each student’s goals

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Visual routine and materials list (math folder, laptop, pencil) posted on the board
  • Group 1 greeted individually at the start of their block, modeled with the AAC device
  • Timer displayed on the board during Group 2's snack/break
  • Clear transition between groups
  • Calm, patient environment maintained during AAC modeling and mindfulness video

Activating Prior Knowledge

The familiar Money Monday routine activates prior knowledge about coins and money. Group 2's check activity reviews the check-writing skill introduced the previous week. The banking theme connects mathematics to meaningful real-world financial tasks.

Materials Needed

Math folder, projector, YouTube, Canva slideshow, progress monitoring for each student, fake money, money Monday warm-up, AAC device, student laptops, teacher laptop, pencil, timer

Vocabulary

Money; coin; value; cents; dollar; count; total; deposit; check; amount; progress monitoring.

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1 — 45 minutes
  • Materials
  • Mindfulness
  • Expectations
  • ASL “deposit”
  • Money Monday coin-counting
  • Progress monitoring
  • Group 2 — 45 minutes
  • 10-minute snack/leisure
  • Materials
  • Mindfulness
  • Expectations
  • ASL “deposit”
  • Money Monday word problem + check
  • Progress monitoring

Closure

  • Group 1: Teacher briefly reviews the coin-counting skill practiced during Money Monday and asks students to identify or communicate one money skill they practiced.
  • Group 2: Teacher reviews the money word problem and check-writing activity and asks students to identify one skill they practiced.
  • Connection to Next Lesson: Teacher connects the lesson to continued work with money, banking, and functional math skills.
