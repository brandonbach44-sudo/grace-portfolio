Patterns & Parking-Lot Word Problems

09/17Counting, Number Sense & DataLife Skills Math

PA Standards

  • PA Core Mathematics CC.2.1.HS.F.6: Extend the knowledge of numbers and their uses to connect mathematics to everyday situations.
  • PA Core Mathematics CC.2.2.HS.D.10: Represent, solve, and interpret real-world problems using mathematical reasoning.
  • Career Education and Work connections: Students practice functional problem solving, following sequential directions, communication, and independent task completion.

Essential Questions

  • How can patterns and problem-solving help me make decisions?
  • How can I use addition and subtraction to solve real-world problems?
  • Why is it important to look carefully at information before choosing an answer?

Objectives

  • By the end of the lesson, students will:
  • Identify what comes next in a visual pattern by examining pictures and selecting the correct next item.
  • Use addition and subtraction to solve a functional word problem involving cars in a parking lot.
  • Combine quantities to determine a total when two groups of cars are added together.
  • Use subtraction to determine an unknown quantity when a total and one quantity are provided.
  • Communicate answers using speech, AAC, gestures, pointing, writing, or another established communication method.
  • Follow turn-taking and classroom routines while participating in the warm-up.
  • Demonstrate approximately 80% accuracy, with individualized prompting and accommodations as appropriate

Assessment

  • Teacher observation during the picture-pattern warm-up, including number/visual recognition, reasoning, and response selection.
  • Student responses to pattern questions and ability to identify the item that comes next.
  • Observation of students solving the car word problem using addition and subtraction.
  • Students' ability to combine red and white cars and identify totals.
  • Students' ability to use subtraction to determine how many red cars are in the parking lot when appropriate information is provided.
  • Observation of communication, turn-taking, attention, independence, and response to prompting.
  • IXL progress-monitoring data collected by the TA during the assigned station.
  • Observation of Digitability participation/navigation and UNO participation, turn-taking, and following directions.

Differentiation

  • For Group 1, use clear picture patterns with a limited number of choices and visual answer options.
  • For students who use AAC, allow students to respond with their AAC device; the teacher may model relevant words or responses on an AAC device.
  • Provide extended processing and wait time before repeating a question or providing a prompt.
  • For Group 2, present the car problem using visual representations of red and white cars so students can connect quantities to the written problem.
  • Break multi-step word problems into smaller steps: identify the information, choose addition or subtraction, solve, and state the answer.
  • Use concrete/visual supports such as drawn cars, counters, or a whiteboard number line when needed.
  • Use least-to-most prompting and fade prompts when students demonstrate independence.
  • Provide individualized IXL assignments and accommodations based on student needs and IEPs.
  • Increase pattern complexity or problem-solving demands for students who demonstrate mastery.
  • Use visual schedules and timers to support transitions and predictability.
  • Provide positive reinforcement, including dollar bills/fake money, for participation, communication, effort, following directions, and increasing independence.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Students enter, gather materials, and follow the posted visual schedule.
  • The teacher reviews expectations and uses the established greeting routine. For Group 1, AAC modeling may be used as appropriate.
  • Students are reminded of the turn-taking expectation: participate when called, listen while peers respond, and wait for the next turn.
  • The teacher provides calm, concise directions and additional processing time as needed.
  • Dollar bills/fake money are used as established positive reinforcement for participation, communication, effort, following directions, and independence.
  • A snack/break occurs between the two groups, followed by a brief transition.
  • A timer on the board is used to make warm-up, station, and transition expectations predictable.
  • During stations, students remain with their assigned adult/activity until the timer or teacher signals a transition.
  • Students receive individualized communication, sensory, behavioral, and instructional supports consistent with their established classroom routines and IEP accommodations.

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • Review that a pattern is something that follows a rule or repeats in a predictable way.
  • Ask students where they see patterns in everyday life, such as clothing, signs, schedules, colors, or classroom routines.
  • Review the meanings of addition (putting groups together) and subtraction (taking away or finding how many are left/different).
  • Connect the car problem to a familiar real-world situation: counting cars in a parking lot.
  • Explain that today's warm-up is Think About It Thursday because students will need to look carefully, think about the information given, and choose a solution.

Materials Needed

  • Math folders
  • Whiteboard and dry-erase markers
  • Projection screen/slideshow
  • Visual schedule
  • AAC devices as applicable
  • FM device as applicable
  • Visual picture-pattern slides/worksheets
  • Car word-problem visual with red and white car representations
  • Timer
  • Dollar bills/fake money for positive reinforcement
  • Laptops
  • IXL
  • Digitability/Bankability
  • UNO cards

Vocabulary

pattern, next, repeat, picture, sequence, rule, addition, add, plus, combine, total, subtraction, subtract, take away, difference, how many, red, white, car, parking lot, answer

Lesson Sequence

  • 0–5 minutes — Entry Routine
  • Students enter, get materials, and review the visual schedule. Teacher greets students and reviews expectations. For Group 1, teacher may model a greeting or functional response using an AAC device as appropriate.
  • 5–10 minutes — Mindfulness / Expectations
  • Complete the established brief mindfulness activity. Review that today's warm-up is Think About It Thursday and remind students to look carefully, think, and take their time before answering.
  • 10–25 minutes — Group 1 Warm-Up: Visual Patterns
  • Display picture patterns on the board. Students examine each pattern and choose what comes next. Teacher models the first example by identifying what repeats or changes. Students take turns responding. Students may come to the board, point to an answer, verbally respond, use AAC, or use another established communication method. Provide extended processing time and least-to-most prompting. Reinforce participation and effort.
  • 25–35 minutes — Group 1 Review / Pattern Questions
  • Review the patterns and ask questions such as: What comes next? What picture repeats? How did you know? Which answer does not belong? Encourage students to explain or demonstrate their reasoning at their individual communication level.
  • 35–45 minutes — Snack / Break / Transition
  • Students have the established snack and break. Teacher prepares materials for the next group.
  • 45–50 minutes — Group 2 Entry Routine
  • Second group enters, follows the visual schedule, gathers materials, and reviews classroom expectations.
  • 50–55 minutes — Mindfulness / Addition and Subtraction Review
  • Complete the established brief mindfulness activity. Review that addition means combining groups and subtraction can be used to take away or find an unknown amount.
  • 55–70 minutes — Group 2 Warm-Up: Car Word Problem
  • Present a visual word problem about red and white cars in a parking lot. Read the problem aloud and display the quantities visually. Guide students through the problem one step at a time: identify the number of red cars, identify the number of white cars, determine whether to add or subtract, solve, and state the answer. Students may use drawings, counters, fingers, a calculator when appropriate, or the teacher's visual model. Ask follow-up questions that require combining groups, such as how many red and white cars there are altogether.
  • 70–80 minutes — Group 2 Problem-Solving Review
  • Review the solution and ask students additional questions about the parking lot. Examples include: How many red cars are there? How many white cars are there? How many red and white cars are there altogether? If some cars leave, how many are left? If you know the total and the number of white cars, how could you find the number of red cars? Use visuals and concrete representations to support students who need them.
  • 80–85 minutes — Daily Station
  • Students complete one assigned station for the day. Station 1: IXL/progress monitoring with a TA. Station 2: Digitability with the teacher, including logging in and practicing the assigned activity. Station 3: UNO with a TA, focusing on turn-taking, following directions, communication, and social interaction. Use the board timer to support station expectations.
  • 85–90 minutes — Closure
  • Review the Think About It Thursday skills. Ask students to identify one pattern they completed or one thing they learned about addition/subtraction. Students respond verbally, with AAC, by pointing, or through another established communication method. Preview the next class routine and reinforce students for participation and effort.
