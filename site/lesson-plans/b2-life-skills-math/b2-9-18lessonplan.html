09/18Life Skills Math

PA Standards

  • PA Core Mathematics CC.2.1.HS.F.6: Extend the knowledge of numbers and their uses to connect mathematics to everyday situations.
  • PA Core Mathematics CC.2.2.HS.D.10: Represent, solve, and interpret real-world problems using mathematical reasoning.
  • Career Education and Work connections: Students practice functional numeracy, financial literacy, communication, decision-making, and independent living skills.

Essential Questions

  • How can I use money and banking skills in everyday life?
  • How can I keep track of money I earn and put it into a bank account?
  • How can I use information about my money to make spending decisions?

Objectives

  • By the end of the lesson, students will:
  • Count money they have earned with an appropriate level of assistance.
  • Record the amount of money earned on a deposit slip with an appropriate level of assistance.
  • Complete a functional banking routine by bringing the deposit slip and earned money to the classroom bank.
  • Participate in entering a cash deposit and the weekly $100 paycheck into their Bankability account.
  • Identify the amount of money available in their account after the deposit and paycheck are entered.
  • Make a personal spending decision by choosing whether or not to shop at the classroom store.
  • Use speech, AAC, gestures, pointing, writing, or another established communication method to participate in the banking routine.
  • Demonstrate increasing independence in functional money and banking tasks, with approximately 80% accuracy or successful completion when individualized supports are provided.

Assessment

  • By the end of the lesson, students will:
  • Count money they have earned with an appropriate level of assistance.
  • Record the amount of money earned on a deposit slip with an appropriate level of assistance.
  • Complete a functional banking routine by bringing the deposit slip and earned money to the classroom bank.
  • Participate in entering a cash deposit and the weekly $100 paycheck into their Bankability account.
  • Identify the amount of money available in their account after the deposit and paycheck are entered.
  • Make a personal spending decision by choosing whether or not to shop at the classroom store.
  • Use speech, AAC, gestures, pointing, writing, or another established communication method to participate in the banking routine.
  • Demonstrate increasing independence in functional money and banking tasks, with approximately 80% accuracy or successful completion when individualized supports are provided.

Differentiation

  • Group 1 students receive more direct support with counting money and completing the deposit slip.
  • Teacher models functional communication throughout the banking routine using an AAC device, while students are encouraged to use their own established communication systems.
  • Provide extended processing and wait time before repeating directions or providing a prompt.
  • Use verbal directions, modeling, visual supports, gestures, and hand-over-hand or physical assistance only when appropriate and consistent with established student supports.
  • Break the banking routine into clear steps: count money, write amount, bring deposit to bank, make deposit, receive account balance, and choose whether to shop.
  • Group 2 students complete the same routine with greater independence and reduced prompting, particularly when counting money and writing the deposit slip.
  • Fade prompts whenever students demonstrate that they can complete a step independently.
  • Use individualized supports and IEP accommodations as appropriate.
  • Allow students to communicate their shopping decision using speech, AAC, pointing, gestures, or another established communication method.
  • Provide access to math games on the computer after students complete the banking routine.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • There is no formal warm-up, sign language routine, or reading of student expectations on this day.
  • Students follow the established classroom entry and transition routines (get all materials, count money).
  • Group 1 students travel to the PACE Center, where the classroom bank is set up for the weekly banking activity.
  • Students complete the banking routine one at a time while other students wait appropriately and remain engaged.
  • After completing their banking task, students transition to a computer and may play an assigned math game while waiting for peers.
  • The teacher provides calm, concise directions and models AAC communication throughout the activity.
  • Students receive positive feedback and reinforcement for participation, communication, effort, following directions, and independence.
  • Group 2 students enter and receive their snack break as part of the established routine before completing the banking activity.
  • The second group completes the same banking routine with an emphasis on increased independence.
  • The teacher maintains a predictable sequence and provides individualized support rather than completing the task for the student.

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • Connect the activity to students' previous experiences with earning money, receiving paychecks, making purchases, and using the classroom store.
  • Review that money can be earned, saved, deposited, and spent.
  • Remind students that their weekly paycheck is $100 and that bills do not need to be paid until next month.
  • Explain that today's activity gives students an opportunity to practice a real-life banking routine and make a choice about how they want to use their available money.
  • Emphasize that students are responsible for counting and recording the money they earned, with support provided as needed.

Materials Needed

  • Math folders
  • PACE Center classroom bank setup
  • Earned classroom money
  • Deposit slips
  • Pencils
  • Computers/laptops
  • Bankability account
  • Classroom store
  • AAC devices
  • Teacher AAC device for modeling
  • Visual supports as needed
  • Math games
  • Snack for Group 2

Vocabulary

money, earn, paycheck, deposit, deposit slip, bank, bank account, cash, balance, total, count, dollar, amount, save, spend, shop, store, choose, more, less

Lesson Sequence

  • Group 1 — Arrival / Transition to PACE Center
  • Students from Group 1 come to the PACE Center and review the banking routine. The teacher explains that students will count the money they earned, complete a deposit slip, and bring it to the classroom bank. There is no formal warm-up, sign language routine, or reading of student expectations.
  • Group 1 — Count Earned Money
  • Students work one at a time to count the money they have earned. The teacher provides assistance as needed, including verbal prompts, modeling, pointing, or other established supports. The teacher models functional AAC communication throughout the activity.
  • Group 1 — Complete Deposit Slip
  • Students record the amount of money they counted on a deposit slip with teacher assistance as needed. The teacher helps students identify where to write the amount and checks that the recorded amount matches the money counted.
  • Group 1 — Make Bank Deposit
  • Students bring their money and deposit slip to the teacher at the classroom bank. Students participate in the deposit routine one at a time. The teacher enters the student's cash deposit into the student's Bankability account and models relevant communication using the AAC device.
  • Group 1 — Add Weekly Paycheck
  • The teacher enters the student's weekly $100 paycheck into the Bankability account. Students are reminded that they do not need to pay bills until next month.
  • Group 1 — Review Account Balance / Make a Choice
  • The teacher tells the student how much money is now in the account. The student is given the choice to shop at the classroom store or choose not to shop. The student communicates the decision through speech, AAC, pointing, gestures, or another established communication method.
  • Group 1 — Independent/Waiting Activity
  • After completing the banking routine, students transition to a computer and may play an assigned math game while waiting for classmates to finish.
  • Group 2 — Arrival / Snack Break
  • The second group enters and completes their established snack break. After snack, students transition to the PACE Center/classroom bank for the weekly banking routine.
  • Group 2 — Count Earned Money
  • Students independently count the money they have earned as much as possible. The teacher provides support only when needed, with the goal of encouraging independence and accurate counting.
  • Group 2 — Complete Deposit Slip
  • Students record the amount they counted on the deposit slip. Students are encouraged to complete as much of the slip independently as possible before receiving a prompt.
  • Group 2 — Make Bank Deposit
  • Students bring their money and deposit slip to the teacher at the bank one at a time. The teacher enters the cash deposit into the student's Bankability account while continuing to model functional AAC communication.
  • Group 2 — Add Weekly Paycheck / Review Balance
  • The teacher enters the weekly $100 paycheck, explains the student's updated account balance, and reminds students that bills do not need to be paid until next month.
  • Group 2 — Make a Shopping Decision
  • Students decide whether they want to shop at the classroom store. The teacher supports students in communicating their choice and reinforces independent decision-making

Closure

Review the purpose of the banking activity: count what you earned, record the amount, deposit the money, receive the weekly paycheck, check the account balance, and decide whether to spend or save. Students communicate one part of the routine they completed or demonstrate their choice about shopping. Students are positively reinforced for participation, communication, effort, and independence.
