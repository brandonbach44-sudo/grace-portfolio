Community Grocery Trip: Comparing Prices

B2_9-22LessonPlan.docx

Also in: Block 2 Math/Week 4/Lesson Plans

09/22Money & Banking · Cooking & Community SkillsLife Skills Math (CBI)

PA Standards

PA Career Education and Work Standard 13.3.11.B: Analyze consumer skills, including comparison shopping and unit pricing, needed for independent living.

Essential Questions

  • How can we compare prices to find the best value when shopping?
  • What steps are involved in successfully completing a shopping trip from start to finish?

Objectives

  • Students will locate and select their assigned grocery items using a picture list.
  • Students will (as appropriate to individual goals) compare size and price to select the best-value item.
  • Students will use appropriate social skills at checkout, including greeting the cashier and bagging items.
  • Students will safely walk to and from the store as part of the group.
  • Students will correctly put away purchased items in their designated spot in the school kitchen.

Assessment

  • Checklist: student successfully located and selected all 3 assigned items
  • For students working on price comparison: correct selection of the best-value item based on size/price
  • Adult observation of safety and behavior during the walk, in-store, and at checkout
  • Correct placement of items in designated kitchen storage upon return

Differentiation

  • Each student paired 1:1 with an adult for support throughout the trip
  • Picture-based grocery list provided for students who are non-readers or benefit from visual supports
  • Price/size comparison introduced as an extension goal for students working on that skill; other students focus on locating and selecting their items
  • Communication supports (AAC device, visual cue cards) available as needed for checkout interactions
  • Trip broken into clear, sequential steps with a visual schedule to support predictability

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Safety expectations and walking route reviewed with all students before leaving the building
  • Students divided into small groups, each with an assigned adult for the entire trip
  • Visual schedule of the CBI trip sequence reviewed before departure (walk to Giant → shop → checkout → walk back → put away)
  • Clear meeting point established (registers) for regrouping after shopping
  • Store behavior expectations reviewed (indoor voice, staying with adult, respectful interactions with staff/other shoppers)

Activating Prior Knowledge

(0-5 min) Review the visual schedule for today's CBI trip to Giant. Assign students to groups/adults and hand out each student's picture grocery list (3 items per student). Review safety expectations for the walk and appropriate behavior in the store.

Materials Needed

  • Picture grocery list for each student (3 items per list)
  • Visual schedule of the CBI trip
  • Payment method (school funds/card or cash)
  • Reusable bags
  • AAC devices/communication supports as needed
  • Kitchen storage labels/guide for correct put-away location

Vocabulary

  • Compare — looking at two or more items to decide which is better
  • Unit price — the cost per size/amount of an item, used to compare value
  • Best value/best buy — the item that gives the most for the lowest price
  • Greet — to say hello or acknowledge someone politely

Lesson Sequence

  • (5-20 min) Walk to Giant as a group, with students remaining with their assigned adult.
  • (20-45 min) Shopping: in small groups, each student locates their 3 assigned items using their picture list. Adults support as needed. For students working on comparison shopping, adults prompt them to look at price and size to choose the cheaper option of the correct size.
  • (45-50 min) Students and adults meet at the registers with their items.
  • (50-55 min) Checkout: students place their items on the belt, greet the cashier, and bag their items with adult support as needed.
  • (55-70 min) Walk back to school as a group, with students remaining with their assigned adult.
  • (70-85 min) Students put their purchased items away in the correct designated spot in the school kitchen.

Closure

(85-90 min) Gather as a class to briefly discuss the trip: what items did you find, did anyone compare prices, what went well or felt challenging? Preview how these items may be used in an upcoming cooking/kitchen lesson.
