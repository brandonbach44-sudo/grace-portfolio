Counting in Order: Dot-to-Dot

09/16Counting, Number Sense & DataLife Skills Math

PA Standards

  • PA Core Academic Standards for Mathematics, CC.2.1.HS.F.6 — Extend the knowledge of numbers and their uses to connect mathematics to everyday situations.
  • PA Core Academic Standards for Mathematics, CC.2.2.HS.D.10 — Represent, solve, and interpret real-world problems using mathematical reasoning.
  • Career Education and Work connections: students practice functional numeracy, following sequential directions, communication, and independence skills that can be applied to school, work, and daily routines.

Essential Questions

  • How can I use numbers to count and complete a task?
  • Why is it important to count in the correct order?
  • How can counting and recognizing numbers help me in everyday life

Objectives

  • By the end of the lesson, students will:
  • Demonstrate one-to-one counting by counting numbers in sequence during the dot-to-dot activity.
  • Identify and connect numbers in the correct numerical order.
  • Follow a turn-taking routine by connecting three dots before allowing the next student to participate.
  • Answer teacher questions about the picture created by the dot-to-dot activity.
  • Participate in functional math activities using speech, AAC, gestures, pointing, or another established communication method.
  • Demonstrate the targeted counting/numeracy skill with at least 80% accuracy, with individualized prompting and accommodations as needed.
  • Participate appropriately and work toward increased independence during the daily station.

Assessment

  • Students will show what they know by:
  • Teacher observation of students counting and identifying numbers during the dot-to-dot warm-up.
  • Observation of students connecting the correct three dots in numerical sequence.
  • Student responses to questions about the completed picture, such as what the picture is, how many objects they see, or what numbers they connected.
  • Teacher observation of communication, turn-taking, attention to task, and independence.
  • IXL progress-monitoring data collected by the TA.
  • Observation of students independently logging into and navigating Digitability.
  • Observation of appropriate participation, turn-taking, and following directions during UNO.
  • Brief individual response or demonstration during closure.

Differentiation

  • First-group students will have access to AAC devices throughout instruction to support communication.
  • Provide longer processing/wait time before repeating questions or prompting responses.
  • Model counting and expected responses using speech, AAC, gestures, and visual supports.
  • Allow students to demonstrate understanding by pointing, counting verbally, using AAC, connecting dots, gesturing, writing, or otherwise responding through their established communication method.
  • Use the 1–10 car dot-to-dot for the first group to provide a shorter, concrete counting sequence.
  • Use the 1–36 city dot-to-dot for the second group to provide increased numerical range and challenge.
  • Break the task into manageable turns of three dots at a time.
  • Provide visual and verbal prompts as needed and use least-to-most prompting.
  • Allow additional time for students who need it to locate and connect the correct number.
  • Use the visual schedule and timer to support transitions and predictability.
  • Provide individualized IXL work and progress monitoring according to each student’s instructional level.
  • For students demonstrating mastery, ask additional questions involving counting, quantity, sequencing, or details in the completed picture.
  • Provide adult assistance and accommodations required by student IEPs.
  • Reinforce participation, communication, independence, and effort using the classroom dollar-bill reward system.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Students enter, read the visual schedule, collect their materials, and move to their appropriate work area.
  • Teacher greets students individually and uses AAC with the first group to model functional communication.
  • Students review classroom expectations before instruction.
  • Dollar bills are provided as positive reinforcement for participation, communication, following directions, and completing classroom routines.
  • Students have a predictable snack and break between the first and second groups.
  • The visual schedule and timer on the board communicate transitions and station time.
  • During the dot-to-dot activity, students come to the board one at a time and connect three dots before the next student takes a turn.
  • Students remain in their seats and attend to the activity while waiting for their turn.
  • During stations, students remain with their assigned adult/activity until the timer signals the transition.
  • Adults use calm, concise directions and allow additional processing time when needed.
  • Students may use established communication, sensory, and regulation supports consistent with their individual plans.
  • Adults provide specific positive feedback and redirect students to the visual schedule or task when needed.

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • Begin by reviewing numbers and counting in sequence.
  • Ask students to count together as a group using their preferred communication method.
  • Show students the dot-to-dot activity and explain that the numbers must be connected in the correct order to reveal a picture.
  • Connect counting to familiar everyday activities such as counting money, items, steps, classroom materials, or days on a calendar.
  • Build motivation by explaining that each student will contribute to creating a picture for the class.
  • Preview that students will take turns connecting three dots before the next student continues.

Materials Needed

Math folders, laptops, whiteboard, whiteboard markers, projection screen, math slideshow/visual examples, visual schedule, AAC devices, FM device as applicable, first-group 1–10 car dot-to-dot, second-group 1–36 city dot-to-dot, classroom timer, dollar bills/fake dollar bills for participation, IXL, Digitability, and UNO cards.

Vocabulary

Number, count, counting, sequence, number order, before, after, next, dot, connect, picture, quantity, more, less, turn, city, car.

Lesson Sequence

  • 0–5 minutes — Entry Routine: Students enter, check the visual schedule, collect materials, and sit in their work area. Teacher greets students individually. With the first group, teacher uses AAC to model greetings and functional communication. Dollar bills are provided for participation and completion of the entry routine.
  • 5–10 minutes — Mindfulness/Expectations: Complete the usual mindfulness routine. Review classroom expectations and allow students to participate using speech, AAC, gestures, or another established method. Provide longer processing time for the first group.
  • 10–25 minutes — What’s the Number Wednesday Warm-Up, First Group: Display the 1–10 dot-to-dot car picture on the whiteboard. Review the numbers and explain that students will connect the dots in numerical order to reveal the picture. Students come to the board one at a time and connect three dots. The next student then connects the next three dots. Continue until the picture is completed. Teacher supports students with counting, number identification, and sequencing as needed. Students waiting for their turn remain engaged by watching for the next number. Use AAC and extended processing time for communication. Provide dollar bills for participation and effort.
  • 25–35 minutes — Picture Questions/Review: After the car is completed, ask students questions about their drawing. Questions may include: “What did we make?”, “What number did you connect?”, “What number comes next?”, and “How many numbers did we use?” Students respond using speech, AAC, pointing, gestures, or another established method. Teacher provides feedback and reteaches number sequence when needed.
  • 35–45 minutes — Snack and Break/Transition: Students clean up, transition according to the visual schedule, and participate in snack. Students then have a short break and may choose an appropriate activity. The timer signals when students should return.
  • 45–50 minutes — Second Group Entry Routine: Second group enters, checks the visual schedule, gathers materials, and completes the snack/break routine as needed. Teacher reviews expectations and provides dollar bills for appropriate participation.
  • 50–55 minutes — Mindfulness/Counting Review: Complete the usual mindfulness routine and briefly review counting and number order. Count together and explain that today’s activity will use numbers 1–36.
  • 55–70 minutes — What’s the Number Wednesday Warm-Up, Second Group: Display the 1–36 city dot-to-dot on the whiteboard. Explain that students will work together to connect the numbers in numerical order. Students come to the board one at a time and connect three dots, then the next student connects the following three. Continue until the city picture is completed. Teacher monitors number identification, counting, sequencing, and appropriate turn-taking.
  • Provide individualized prompts and dollar-bill reinforcement for participation.
  • 70–80 minutes — Picture Questions/Review: Ask students questions about the completed city drawing. Questions may include: “What picture did we make?”, “What number comes after ___?”, “What was the highest number we connected?”, “How many dots did we use?”, or questions about objects/details visible in the picture. Adjust questions to each student’s level.
  • 80–85 minutes — Daily Station: Because this is a shorter class, students complete one station for the day. The timer on the board communicates the station work period.
  • IXL/Progress Monitoring: Students work with a TA on individualized IXL activities and progress monitoring. The TA records relevant data.
  • Digitability: Students meet with the teacher to log into Digitability, practice navigating their account, and complete the assigned activity.
  • UNO: Students work with a TA to play UNO while practicing turn-taking, following directions, communication, and social interaction.
  • Students are assigned to a station based on instructional needs. Adults monitor participation, provide prompts and feedback, and reinforce independence.
  • 85–90 minutes —

Closure

  • Review the importance of counting and putting numbers in the correct order. Ask each student to identify a number, state or demonstrate what number comes next, or answer a question about the picture based on their instructional level. Reinforce appropriate turn-taking and participation. Preview that the next lesson will continue practicing functional math and number skills.
