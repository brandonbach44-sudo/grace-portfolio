Telling Time & Elapsed Time

09/15Time & MeasurementLife Skills Math

PA Standards

  • PA Core Academic Standards for Mathematics, CC.2.1.HS.F.6 — Extend the knowledge of numbers and their uses to connect mathematics to everyday situations.
  • PA Core Academic Standards for Mathematics, CC.2.2.HS.D.10 — Represent, solve, and interpret real-world problems using mathematical reasoning.
  • Career Education and Work connections: students practice functional time-management and independence skills that can be applied to school, work, appointments, and daily routines

Essential Questions

  • How can I use a clock to tell what time it is?
  • How can I figure out how much time has passed?
  • Why is knowing and managing time an important everyday skill?

Objectives

  • By the end of the lesson, students will:
  • Read and identify times shown on an analog clock.
  • Match an example time by moving the hands on a Judy Clock.
  • For students working on elapsed time, determine how much time has passed between a beginning and ending time using a Judy Clock.
  • Participate using speech, AAC, sign language, pointing, writing, or another established communication method.
  • Demonstrate the targeted time skill with at least 80% accuracy, with individualized prompting and accommodations as needed.
  • Practice independence, attention to task, and appropriate participation during the daily station.

Assessment

  • Students will show what they know by:
  • Teacher observation while students identify and match times on the Judy Clock.
  • Students moving Judy Clock hands to match an example clock.
  • Responses to questions about the hour, minute, beginning time, ending time, and elapsed time.
  • Individualized elapsed-time questions during the second group’s warm-up.
  • Teacher observation of independence, prompting level, and accuracy during the station.
  • IXL progress-monitoring data collected by the TA.
  • Digitability login/navigation performance with the teacher.
  • Appropriate participation and turn-taking during the UNO station.
  • Brief response or demonstration during closure.

Differentiation

  • First-group students will have access to AAC devices throughout instruction.
  • Provide longer processing/wait time before repeating questions or prompting responses.
  • Model responses using AAC, speech, gestures, and the Judy Clock.
  • Allow students to demonstrate understanding by moving clock hands, pointing, using AAC, gesturing, writing, or verbally responding.
  • Use concrete Judy Clocks and visual examples rather than relying only on verbal explanations.
  • Break directions into one step at a time and check understanding before adding another step.
  • Provide repeated practice and least-to-most prompting as needed.
  • Use the visual schedule and timer to support transitions and predictability.
  • Provide individualized IXL work and progress monitoring according to each student’s level.
  • For students who demonstrate mastery, provide more complex elapsed-time questions or larger intervals.
  • Adjust the number and difficulty of questions based on individual readiness.
  • Provide adult assistance and accommodations required by student IEPs.
  • Reinforce participation, communication, independence, and effort with the classroom dollar-bill reward system.

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Students enter, read the visual schedule, collect materials, and move to their work area.
  • Teacher greets students individually and uses AAC with the first group to model functional communication.
  • Students review classroom expectations before instruction.
  • Dollar bills are provided as positive reinforcement for participation, communication, following directions, and completing routines.
  • Students have a predictable snack and break between the first and second groups.
  • The visual schedule and timer on the board communicate transitions and station time.
  • During the station, students remain with their assigned adult/activity until the timer signals the transition.
  • Adults use calm, concise directions and allow additional processing time when needed.
  • Students may use established communication, sensory, and regulation supports consistent with their plans.
  • Adults provide specific positive feedback and redirect students to the visual schedule or task when needed.
  • Students can independently access clocks, laptops, communication devices, and station materials.

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • Show an analog clock and ask students what they notice.
  • Review the hour hand and minute hand using the Judy Clock.
  • Connect telling time to familiar routines such as arrival, snack, lunch, class changes, break, and going home.
  • Explain that the first group will practice matching displayed times.
  • Explain that the second group will build on telling-time skills by figuring out elapsed time.
  • Emphasize that knowing time helps students become more independent at school, work, and in the community.

Materials Needed

Math folders, laptops, whiteboard, markers, projection screen, math slideshow/visual examples, visual schedule, AAC devices, FM device as applicable, Judy Clocks, example analog clock, elapsed-time questions, classroom timer, dollar bills/fake dollar bills, IXL, Digitability, and UNO cards.

Vocabulary

Time, clock, analog clock, hour hand, minute hand, hour, minute, beginning time, ending time, elapsed time, before, after, earlier, later, o’clock, half hour, quarter hour.

Lesson Sequence

  • 0–5 minutes — Entry Routine: Students enter, check the visual schedule, collect materials, and sit in their work area. Teacher greets students individually. With the first group, teacher uses AAC to model greetings and functional communication. Dollar bills are provided for participation and completion of the entry routine.
  • 5–10 minutes — Mindfulness/Expectations: Complete the usual mindfulness routine. Review classroom expectations and allow students to participate using speech, AAC, gestures, or another established method. Provide longer processing time for the first group.
  • 10–25 minutes — Time Warm-Up, First Group: Display an example analog clock showing a specific time. Students look at the example and move the hands on their Judy Clocks to match it. Begin with familiar times and adjust difficulty individually. Ask students to identify the hour and minute when appropriate. Provide AAC support, visual cues, modeling, and extended processing time. Distribute dollar bills for participation and effort.
  • 25–35 minutes — Individualized Time Practice: Continue practicing telling time with students who need additional support. Students demonstrating independence receive additional times to match. Teacher circulates, checks clock placement, provides feedback, and notes accuracy/prompting levels.
  • 35–45 minutes — Snack and Break/Transition: Students clean up, transition according to the visual schedule, and participate in snack. Students then have a short break and may choose an appropriate activity. The timer signals when students should return.
  • 45–50 minutes — Second Group Entry Routine: Second group enters, checks the visual schedule, gathers materials, and completes the snack/break routine as needed. Teacher reviews expectations and provides dollar bills for appropriate participation.
  • 50–55 minutes — Mindfulness/Review: Complete the usual mindfulness routine and briefly review the hour hand, minute hand, and reading an analog clock. Introduce elapsed time as the amount of time that passes from a beginning time to an ending time.
  • 55–65 minutes — Time Warm-Up, Second Group: Present elapsed-time questions using Judy Clocks. Give a beginning and ending time. Students move the Judy Clock forward and determine how many minutes or hours passed. Adjust complexity individually. Use concrete modeling, visual supports, verbal cues, and wait time. Reinforce participation with dollar bills.
  • 65–85 minutes — Daily Station: Because this is a shorter class, students complete one station for the day. The timer on the board communicates the work period.
  • IXL/Progress Monitoring: Students work with a TA on individualized IXL activities and progress monitoring. The TA records relevant data.
  • Digitability: Students meet with the teacher to log into Digitability, practice navigating their account, and complete the assigned activity.
  • UNO: Students work with a TA to play UNO while practicing turn-taking, following directions, communication, and social interaction.
  • Students are assigned to a station based on instructional needs. Adults monitor participation, provide prompts and feedback, and reinforce independence.
  • 85–90 minutes —

Closure

  • Review the day’s time skill. Students identify a time, match a displayed time, or demonstrate an elapsed-time answer based on their instructional level. Ask why knowing time is useful in everyday life. Review the station routine and preview the next lesson.
