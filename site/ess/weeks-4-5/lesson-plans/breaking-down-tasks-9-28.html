Mindfulness & Breaking Down Tasks

Breaking Down Tasks 9_28.docx

Emotional Awareness →
09/28Organization & Self-Advocacy · Social & Emotional SkillsEssential Skills and Strategies (ESS)

PA Standards

  • PA SEL Competency — Self-Management: Manage stress and use organizational/task-planning strategies (e.g., mindfulness, breaking tasks into steps) to reach goals.
  • PA Career Education and Work Standard 13.3.11.B: Analyze time-management and task-organization strategies needed for academic and personal success.

Essential Questions

  • How can mindfulness strategies help us clear our minds and calm our thoughts?
  • How can breaking a task into smaller steps make it more manageable?

Objectives

  • Students will practice a mindfulness/meditation strategy to calm their thoughts, reviewing content from the prior week's emotional awareness lesson.
  • Students will verbally break down a familiar task (making a PB&J, choosing an outfit) into manageable steps.
  • Students will identify the purpose and benefits of breaking tasks into smaller steps.
  • Students will identify at least two strategies for breaking down tasks (e.g., small due dates, timers, one step at a time, checklists).
  • Students will independently break down a task of their choice using a Canvas quick check.

Assessment

  • Participation in the meditation video
  • Verbal participation in the group task-breakdown activity (PB&J / outfit)
  • Participation in the discussion of task-breakdown tips
  • Completed Canvas quick check (task breakdown of choice)

Differentiation

  • Visual schedule and materials list posted on the board for predictability
  • Choice of warm-up activity to support engagement
  • Familiar, low-stakes tasks (PB&J, choosing an outfit) used to model task breakdown before applying the skill independently
  • Choice of task on the Canvas quick check to increase relevance and engagement
  • Teacher support available during the Canvas quick check as needed

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Visual schedule and list of needed materials displayed on the board
  • Calm, quiet environment maintained during the meditation video
  • Consistent 'Mindfulness Monday' routine established at the start of each week
  • Clear verbal and visual cues signal transitions between segments

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • (0-10 min) Students enter, follow the schedule and materials list on the board, complete their daily check-in, and do a warm-up activity of their choice.
  • (10-15 min) Mindfulness Monday: 5-minute meditation video, introduced as a strategy for clearing the mind and calming thoughts, reviewing the concept from the prior week's emotional awareness lesson.

Materials Needed

  • Visual schedule and materials list (board/projector)
  • Daily check-in sheet or digital tool
  • Warm-up activity choices
  • Meditation video
  • Task-breakdown discussion prompts (making a PB&J, choosing an outfit)
  • Slide deck: purpose of breaking tasks down + tips and strategies
  • Video on how to break tasks down
  • Canvas quick check (task breakdown of choice)
  • Homework materials

Vocabulary

  • Mindfulness — paying attention to the present moment to calm the mind
  • Task breakdown — splitting a big task into smaller, manageable steps
  • Checklist — a list of steps to track progress on a task

Lesson Sequence

  • (15-23 min) Group activity: students verbally break down two familiar tasks — making a PB&J and choosing an outfit for the day — into smaller, manageable steps.
  • (23-28 min) Slide 1 — Purpose: introduce why we break tasks down: turns something BIG into small, easy steps; can make projects/homework/tasks easier; allows you to manage your time.
  • (28-35 min) Play a video on how to break tasks down, including tips and strategies.
  • (35-45 min) Discuss tips as a class: make yourself small due dates; set yourself timers; only focus on one step at a time; make yourself a checklist.
  • (45-60 min) Students complete a short Canvas quick check, breaking down an activity of their own choice into steps.

Closure

  • (60-90 min) Remaining time: students work on homework.
  • Briefly recap that today's mindfulness practice and task-breakdown strategies are both tools they can use whenever something feels overwhelming.
