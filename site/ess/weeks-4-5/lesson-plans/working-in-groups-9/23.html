Wednesday: Working in Groups Daily Check-In Warm-up: wordle, contexto, sets game Group activity: Pass the conversation ball make sure everyone is listening to one another and taking turns "We just did a fun warm-up getting to know each other a little better, but that conversation ball actually connects to something really important we're going to talk about today: working in groups. No matter what you choose to do after high school, whether you go to college, go straight into a job, or live on your own in the community, you are going to need to work in groups and collaborate with other people. This isn't just a 'school skill.' It shows up everywhere: In school — group projects, class discussions, working with classmates, sports teams, clubs At work — teaming up with coworkers, taking direction from a boss, working alongside customers or clients In the community — living with roommates, working with neighbors, being part of clubs, teams, or community groups So today, we're going to focus on four key things that help us work well in a group, no matter which setting we're in: Listen first — look toward the person talking, and don't start talking over them Wait for a turn — let one person talk at a time Stay on topic — say something that connects to what's already being discussed Share, don't take over — say your piece, then let someone else have a turn you actually already practiced these four skills without even realizing it, during the conversation ball warm-up. Every time you waited for the ball, listened to the person holding it, and then added something that connected to what they said, you were using these exact group skills. That's the goal for today: to notice these skills when we're using them, and to start thinking about how you'll use them not just here in class, but in whatever comes next after high school: college, a job, or life in your community." "Let's make this a little more real, here are some examples of what working in a group might actually look like in each of these settings. In school: Working with a partner or small group on a class project Being part of a class discussion where everyone takes turns sharing ideas Working with a lab partner in science class At work: Splitting up tasks with coworkers to get a job done faster Listening to feedback or instructions from a supervisor Working as a team during a busy shift — like at a restaurant, store, or warehouse In the community: Living with a roommate and figuring out chores or shared space together Being part of a team, club, or volunteer group Working with neighbors on something like a block cleanup or a shared project Notice something ,in every single one of these examples, you'd need to use our four skills: listening first, waiting for your turn, staying on topic, and sharing without taking over. It doesn't matter if you're in a classroom, on a job site, or just living your life in your community , these skills go with you everywhere."
