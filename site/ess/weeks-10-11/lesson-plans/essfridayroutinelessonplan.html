Essential Skills and Strategies (ESS)

PA Standards

  • PA Career Education and Work Standard 13.3.11.B: Analyze the role of personal management skills (e.g., organization, responsibility, self-advocacy) in achieving personal and academic success.
  • Note: verify alignment with individual students' IEP goals related to executive functioning/organization, since this is a recurring routine-based skill-building period rather than new academic content.

Essential Questions

  • How does staying organized and on top of responsibilities help me succeed in and out of school?
  • What strategies can I use to manage my time, my materials, and my responsibilities independently?

Objectives

  • Students will complete a daily check-in routine.
  • Students will identify whether they have missing assignments and use class time to complete them.
  • Students will organize their binder, backpack, and/or locker using an organizational system when materials need it.
  • Students will demonstrate appropriate, responsible use of free-choice time once responsibilities are complete.

Assessment

  • Completion of daily check-in
  • Participation in the group engagement activity
  • Missing-work completion log/tracking
  • Binder/backpack/locker organization checklist
  • Teacher observation of on-task behavior and appropriate choices during work/free-choice time

Differentiation

  • Visual schedule and routine chart posted and referenced daily for predictability
  • Individualized, up-to-date missing-assignment list provided for each student
  • Choice of organizational tools/systems (folders, color-coding, checklists, digital planner) based on student need
  • Clear, posted expectations and modeled examples for appropriate free-choice activities (voice level, sharing, cleanup)
  • Teacher check-ins throughout the period to support self-monitoring and decision-making about which task to prioritize
  • Task-card or checklist system showing the sequence of steps (check-in → group activity → missing work check → organize/work/free choice) for students who benefit from concrete sequencing

Classroom Environment & Routines

  • Visual schedule/routine posted and used daily so the structure is predictable
  • Consistent daily routine: check-in → group engagement activity → missing work check → organize/work/free choice time → closure
  • Positive reinforcement for completing responsibilities (check-in, missing work, organization) before accessing free-choice time
  • Established norms for free-choice/game time: respectful volume, sharing materials, cleaning up before the period ends
  • Teacher circulates throughout independent work time to check in, redirect, and support decision-making

Activating Prior Knowledge

  • (0-5 min) Students enter, review the visual schedule, and complete their daily check-in.
  • (5-20 min) Whole-group engagement activity: Wordle, Contexto, or the Sets game, played and discussed together to start class with a shared, low-pressure warm-up.

Materials Needed

  • Visual schedule/routine chart
  • Daily check-in sheet or digital tool
  • Wordle/Contexto/Sets game (board or device access)
  • Individual missing-assignment list per student
  • Organizational supplies: folders, binders, labels, organization checklists
  • Board/card games for free-choice time (Uno, Monopoly, etc.)

Vocabulary

  • Self-management — the ability to monitor and take responsibility for your own tasks and behavior
  • Prioritize — deciding what needs to be done first
  • Organizational system — a consistent method for keeping materials in order (e.g., folders, labels, checklists)
  • Accountability — following through on responsibilities

Lesson Sequence

  • (20-25 min) Missing work check: students individually review their missing-assignment list to determine whether they have work to complete.
  • (25-80 min) Work time, based on individual need:
  • If a student has missing work, they use this time to complete it, with teacher support available.
  • If a student is caught up but their binder, backpack, or locker needs organizing, they use this time to organize using the checklist/system provided.
  • If a student has completed both missing work and organization, they may choose an appropriate game (Uno, Monopoly, etc.) to play independently or with a peer.
  • (80-88 min) Work time continues; teacher circulates to check progress, redirect as needed, and confirm students have appropriately completed responsibilities before choosing free time.

Closure

  • Students clean up any games or materials used during the period.
  • Quick check-out: each student shares one thing they accomplished today (verbally or on an exit slip) — completed an assignment, organized materials, etc.
  • Preview that tomorrow will follow the same daily routine.
